## 1. EU DPP system architecture: what sits where (CIRPASS / CIRPASS-2 / Commission)

### Takeaway
The EU design is a federated pointer architecture: passport data stays with the responsible economic operator (rEO) or its DPP service provider, with a mandatory mirrored back-up at an independent provider; the Commission runs only a central registry of identifiers plus a web portal that acts as resolver of last resort. As of November 2026 the registry is live (since 20 July 2026), all eight horizontal JTC 24 standards are published, but the delegated act on DPP service providers and the first product-specific delegated acts are still outstanding, so the system is built but mostly empty.

### Cited Findings
- Legal basis: ESPR, Regulation (EU) 2024/1781; DPP rules for batteries come separately from the Battery Regulation (EU) 2023/1542. ESPR is technology-neutral: it requires open standards, interoperability, authentication, integrity, security and privacy without prescribing a technology — [dppgrid (secondary)](https://dppgrid.com/resources/blockchain-product-passport); [dpp-tool (secondary)](https://dpp-tool.com/en/blog/dpp-blockchain/)
- Registry status: IN PRODUCTION. The Commission launched the DPP Registry together with a testing environment on 20 July 2026 (one source gives 19 July, which was the ESPR legal deadline) — [European Commission news, 20 July 2026](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/news/digital-product-passport-registry-now-live-2026-07-20_en?prefLang=ro); [Commission DPP registry page](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/single-market/digital-product-passport/dpp-registry_en)
- Registry legal act: Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1778, adopted 16 July 2026, in force 6 August 2026 — [Regen Studio (secondary)](https://www.regenstudio.world/blog/dpp-registry-goes-live/); [fn.legal (secondary)](https://fn.legal/en/implementing-regulation-on-the-product-passport-register/)
- What the registry holds: it is "a pointer and identifier store rather than a passport database". A verified economic operator submits the unique product identifier and, where relevant, model and batch identifiers, the commodity code, and the reference to the back-up held by a DPP service provider. The registry returns a unique registration identifier, which is explicitly not proof of compliance — [PassportCraft (secondary)](https://passportcraft.com/insights/eu-dpp-registry-who-must-register); [Regen Studio (secondary)](https://www.regenstudio.world/blog/dpp-registry-goes-live/)
- Who can write to the registry: only verified operators; identity must be proven first, with the proof depending on business type. Registration is via web interface or API, and can be delegated to a verified third party / authorised DPP service provider (Implementing Regulation Art. 8 and 19(4) as cited by the source) — [Regen Studio (secondary)](https://www.regenstudio.world/blog/dpp-registry-goes-live/); [Renoon (secondary)](https://www.renoon.com/blog/eu-digital-product-passport-dpp-registry-explained-upcoming-requirements-for-market-access)
- Decentralised storage: product information is stored in a decentralised manner while the registry records unique product identifiers and mandatory metadata — [EU transition-pathways news](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/retail/news/digital-product-passport-registry-now-live)
- Resolver logic (CIRPASS-2): in normal operation the rEO's redirection service, typically run by its DPP service provider, routes the user from the data carrier to the live DPP; the EU Web Portal is the "always-on resolver of last resort" — [smartlinks summary of CIRPASS-2 D4.1 (secondary)](https://www.smartlinks.app/resources/cirpass-reference-architecture)
- Back-up: CIRPASS-2 guidance is that back-up providers mirror the full DPP dataset, stay synchronised with the primary system and apply the same access controls — [WIoT on CIRPASS-2 (secondary)](https://wiot-group.com/think/en/news/cirpass-2-eu-dpp-reference-architecture/)
- CIRPASS-2 reference architecture D4.1 v1.1 published June 2026 (sources give 9, 10 or 17 June), with a risks document; on 16 June 2026 CIRPASS-2 released an open-source test environment including a mock EU registry and validation tools. The authors describe the architecture as an exploratory proposal, not a binding specification. Status: PUBLISHED, non-normative — [CIRPASS-2 project results](https://cirpass2.eu/project-results/); [eudigitalproductpassport.org (secondary)](https://eudigitalproductpassport.org/updates/cirpass-2-reference-architecture-test-environment)
- The CIRPASS-2 architecture recommends Verifiable Credentials for organisational identities, authenticated roles and DPP records — [WIoT on CIRPASS-2 (secondary)](https://wiot-group.com/think/en/news/cirpass-2-eu-dpp-reference-architecture/)
- Predecessor: CIRPASS D3.2 "DPP System Architecture" v1.8, March 2024 (the original decentralised design with registry, resolver, data carrier and access-rights tiers). Status: PUBLISHED, superseded by CIRPASS-2 D4.1 — [CIRPASS D3.2 PDF](https://cirpassproject.eu/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/D3.2-DPP-System-Architecture.pdf)
- Horizontal standards (CEN-CLC/JTC 24), status PUBLISHED: six standards (EN 18216, 18219, 18220, 18221, 18222, 18223) published 27 May 2026 per genorma and CDX, publicly presented 25 June 2026 per WIoT; EN 18239 and EN 18246 published 16 September 2026 — [genorma EN 18216:2026](https://genorma.com/en/standards/en-18216-2026); [WIoT (secondary)](https://wiot-group.com/think/en/news/cen-cenelec-six-eu-dpp-standards-en-18xxx/); [DPPify (secondary)](https://www.dppify.com/blog/dpp-standards-en-18239-18246-published)
- What each standard covers: EN 18216 data exchange protocols and formats; EN 18219 unique identifiers (product, operator, facility); EN 18220 data carriers; EN 18221 storage, archiving, persistence and replication to back-up operators; EN 18222 APIs for finding, resolving, retrieving and managing DPP data over the lifecycle; EN 18223 system interoperability (semantic models, metadata); EN 18239 access-rights management; EN 18246 data authentication, reliability and integrity — [Regen Studio standards overview (secondary)](https://www.regenstudio.world/blog/dpp-system-standards/); [Blippa overview (secondary)](https://blippa.com/en/articles/dpp-standards-overview); [BSI on EN 18221](https://knowledge.bsigroup.com/products/digital-product-passport-data-storage-archiving-and-data-persistence)
- Role-based access (EN 18239): role-based separation between public and restricted (commercially confidential) data, authentication aligned with eIDAS assurance levels, and handover of responsibility between operators — [Trustrace (secondary)](https://trustrace.com/knowledge-hub/the-final-two-european-dpp-standards-are-here-what-it-means-for-fashion-and-apparel-brands)
- Official Journal citation: Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2026/1736 on harmonised standards for the DPP exists (reported date 14 July 2026) and is reported to cite the first six standards; EN 18239 and EN 18246 were not yet cited at the time of the September sources — [EU transition-pathways policy entry](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/retail/policy/commission-implementing-decision-eu-20261736-harmonised-standards-digital-product); [Talkpod (secondary)](https://talkpod.ai/blog/dpp-standards-published/); contradicted in timing by an earlier CDX piece saying citation was still pending — [CDX (secondary)](https://public.cdxsystem.com/en/web/cdx/w/first-european-standards-for-the-digital-product-passport-published)
- DPP service providers: ESPR empowers the Commission to adopt a delegated act on requirements for DPP service providers and a possible certification scheme. Call for evidence closed December 2024; a public consultation followed (Orgalim response dated 1 July 2025). A secondary source citing the Commission timeline places adoption in Q3 2027, with the first product-specific delegated act (iron and steel) in Q4 2026; dates are indicative. Status: DRAFT / not adopted — [Commission call for evidence news, Nov 2024](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/news/commission-seeks-views-future-digital-product-passport-2024-11-13_en); [Renoon (secondary)](https://www.renoon.com/blog/digital-product-passport-latest-updates-the-next-developments-to-watch); [Orgalim](https://orgalim.eu/resource/response-to-the-european-commission-public-consultation-on-digital-product-passport-dpp-service-providers/)
- After a product-specific delegated act, operators generally get at least 18 months before requirements apply — [Renoon (secondary)](https://www.renoon.com/blog/eu-digital-product-passport-dpp-registry-explained-upcoming-requirements-for-market-access)
- Service-provider constraints already in ESPR: a provider is an independent third party authorised by the economic operator; it may not sell, reuse or process DPP data beyond what the service needs unless specifically agreed — [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/digital-product-passports-in-the-eu-comprehensive-expansion)

### Inferences
- Layer map for an architect: (1) data carrier on product encodes a resolvable unique identifier; (2) rEO or provider resolver redirects by identifier and requester role; (3) DPP data store at rEO/provider serves public data openly and restricted data after authentication (EN 18239, EN 18222 APIs); (4) independent back-up provider holds a synchronised mirror (EN 18221); (5) EU registry holds identifier, commodity code and back-up pointer, and is what customs and market surveillance check; (6) EU web portal is the public fallback resolver.
- The registry verifies WHO registered and that an entry exists, not that passport content is true. Content truth is therefore an open layer, which is exactly where a "Know Your Product" verification service would sit.
- Because the service-provider delegated act is not expected before 2027, there is currently no certified class of DPP providers; any provider claim of being "EU-certified" in 2026 is premature.

### Gaps
- The exact field list of Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1778, whether an operator identifier is a separate mandatory field, and the precise access rules for customs and market surveillance authorities could not be confirmed; no source reproduced the legal text.
- One search summary reported a DG GROW user guide dated 24 August 2026 stating that no registration can yet succeed in production; I could not identify or verify the underlying source. Whether real registrations are being accepted as of November 2026 is unresolved.
- No source was found describing concrete "legitimate interest" access rules or CIRPASS-2 pilot results.
- ESPR article numbering for the back-up duty conflicts between sources (Art. 10, 10(4), 11, 27 are all cited); check the Official Journal text.

## 2. Identifiers and data carriers (GS1 Digital Link, Sunrise 2027, ISO/IEC 15459, QR / DataMatrix / NFC / RFID, granularity, JTC 24)

### Takeaway
EN 18219 does not mandate GS1; it admits several identifier schemes anchored on ISO/IEC 15459, with GS1 Digital Link (GTIN plus optional batch and serial in an HTTPS URI) as the de facto retail choice and IEC 61406 identification links common in industrial goods. EN 18220 requires at least one free-to-use, smartphone-readable carrier (in practice a QR code), with DataMatrix, NFC and RFID as permitted alternatives or additions; granularity (model, batch or item) is set per product group by the delegated acts, not by the horizontal standards.

### Cited Findings
- EN 18219 (PUBLISHED 2026) is the identifier layer: it permits five identifier schemes including ISO/IEC 15459 AIDC identifiers, covers product, economic operator and facility identifiers, and defines the model, batch and item levels — [Regen Studio (secondary)](https://www.regenstudio.world/blog/dpp-system-standards/); [opendpp-node guide (secondary)](https://opendpp-node.eu/standards)
- For operator identifiers EN 18219 includes an approach based on ISO/IEC 6523 and lists the LEI (ISO 17442) among example schemes — [GLEIF blog](https://www.gleif.org/en/newsroom/blog/building-trust-in-digital-trade-standards-are-moving-fast-identity-must-keep-up/)
- EN 18220 (PUBLISHED 2026) covers QR and Data Matrix symbols, NFC and RFID; it requires at least one data carrier that is free to use and readable with an ordinary smartphone; one source states QR codes must be encoded in conformance with GS1 Digital Link — [iTeh catalogue entry SIST EN 18220:2026](https://standards.iteh.ai/catalog/standards/sist/45487369-ec3e-47ef-82da-89a412cb2492/sist-en-18220-2026); [idpp.net guide (secondary)](https://idpp.net/en/guides/dpp-standards-guide)
- Conflict: one blog assigns RFID to EN 18221 and DataMatrix/NFC to EN 18222; all other sources contradict this and it should be treated as an error — [qr3.app (secondary, unreliable on this point)](https://qr3.app/en/blog/dpp-en-standards-18216-18223-20260615/)
- GS1 Digital Link: a web URI syntax that carries GS1 keys (GTIN, plus batch/lot and serial qualifiers) so that one 2D code serves both point-of-sale scanning and web resolution to resources such as a DPP or EPCIS history. Status: PUBLISHED GS1 standard, IN PRODUCTION — [GS1 in Europe, "GS1 Standards enabling the EU DPP"](https://gs1.eu/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/GS1-Standards-Enabling-DPP.pdf); [OpenEPCIS FAQ (secondary)](https://openepcis.io/faq/)
- GS1 Sunrise 2027: an industry ambition, not a regulation, that retail point-of-sale scanners can read 2D codes by end of 2027. Sources differ on framing (31 December 2027 deadline vs. "by 2028"); a secondary source says GS1's 2D readiness framework as refreshed 22 September 2026 defines the goal as scanner capability, not codes on shelf. Retail scanning and ESPR run on separate tracks — [Videojet (secondary)](https://www.videojet.com/us/homepage/resources/learn/gs1-sunrise-2027.html); [Unitag blog (secondary)](https://www.unitag.io/blog/gs1-digital-link-vs-qr-code-what-brands-and-printers-need-to-know-before-sunrise-2027/)
- Adoption signal: GS1 UK reports 11% of its 60,000 members live on GS1 Digital Link as of early 2026; the GS1 UK / Tesco pilot is cited as shaping conformance requirements — [Unitag blog (secondary)](https://www.unitag.io/blog/gs1-digital-link-vs-qr-code-what-brands-and-printers-need-to-know-before-sunrise-2027/)
- GS1's own DPP standard is described as provisional and not yet ratified (2026) — [Unitag blog (secondary)](https://www.unitag.io/blog/gs1-digital-link-vs-qr-code-what-brands-and-printers-need-to-know-before-sunrise-2027/)
- GS1 filed formal comments on prEN 18221 in July 2025, i.e. GS1 in Europe participates directly in JTC 24 drafting — [GS1 in Europe comments PDF](https://gs1.eu/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/cen-AprPN-prEN-18221_GS1-comments_07-2025.pdf)
- Industrial alternative: the ZVEI/IDTA "DPP4.0" concept pairs the IEC 61406 identification link (a URL on the nameplate) with the IEC 63278 Asset Administration Shell — [ZVEI DPP4.0 architecture discussion paper](https://www.zvei.org/fileadmin/user_upload/Themen/Industrie/Fachverband_Automation/2023-12_Discussion_Paper_DPP4.0_Architecture_v1.1.pdf)
- RFID implications of the registry rules are discussed in trade press: the registry is carrier-agnostic and keys on the identifier, not the carrier — [RFID News (secondary)](https://www.rfidnews.co.uk/2026/08/19/inside-the-eu-dpp-registry-what-the-new-rules-actually-mean-for-rfid/)

### Inferences
- A KYP layer should treat the identifier as the join key and be carrier-agnostic: accept GS1 Digital Link URIs, IEC 61406 links and other ISO/IEC 15459-rooted identifiers, and normalise them internally.
- Item-level (serialised) granularity is the precondition for authentication and anti-diversion; model-level passports can prove claims about a design but cannot bind to a specific pack. For regulated goods where batch release matters (certificate of analysis per lot), batch level is the minimum useful granularity.
- Since QR is the mandated baseline and is trivially copyable, any anti-counterfeit function must come from a second factor (see section 7).

### Gaps
- Clause-level requirements of EN 18219 (the exact list of five schemes) and EN 18220 (symbol quality, placement, durability) were not verified against the standards' text.
- No source found confirming which granularity the first ESPR delegated acts (iron and steel, textiles) will require.
- GS1's official wording of the Sunrise 2027 goal was only found via vendor blogs.

## 3. Event and traceability data (GS1 EPCIS 2.0 and CBV)

### Takeaway
EPCIS 2.0 is the mature, ISO-standardised vocabulary for "what happened to this object, where, when and why", and is the most plausible source of dynamic passport content; the DPP standards themselves do not mandate it. Events are raw operational records, so they become passport content only after aggregation and, for trust, after being signed or wrapped in credentials.

### Cited Findings
- EPCIS 2.0 is standardised as ISO/IEC 19987:2024 (third edition), with the Core Business Vocabulary as ISO/IEC 19988. Status: PUBLISHED, IN PRODUCTION — [iTeh ISO/IEC 19987:2024](https://standards.iteh.ai/catalog/standards/iso/d799a114-aba0-4b07-a3c4-45ff1edfb30f/iso-iec-19987-2024); [GS1 EPCIS page](https://www.gs1.org/epcis)
- Event types: ObjectEvent, AggregationEvent, TransactionEvent, TransformationEvent and the new AssociationEvent, all subclasses of EPCISEvent — [iTeh ISO/IEC 19987:2024](https://standards.iteh.ai/catalog/standards/iso/d799a114-aba0-4b07-a3c4-45ff1edfb30f/iso-iec-19987-2024)
- CBV supplies standard codes for business steps (e.g. "shipping", "receiving"), dispositions (e.g. "in_transit", "recalled") and location types — [Dublin Core MSI summary of EPCIS](https://msi.dublincore.org/standards/epcis)
- Version 2.0 added JSON / JSON-LD bindings and a REST API alongside XML, plus a SensorElement for readings such as temperature, humidity and position (cold-chain monitoring) — [GS1 EPCIS 2.0 launch deck](https://GS1.org/docs/epcis/epcis_2-0_launch.pdf); [Walmart supplier EPCIS 2.0 docs](https://developer.walmart.com/suppliers/docs/epcis-2-events)
- Real adoption: Walmart publishes EPCIS 2.0 event requirements for suppliers — [Walmart developer docs](https://developer.walmart.com/suppliers/docs/epcis-2-events)
- Layering as implementers describe it: identifiers say which product, EPCIS events record what happened, verifiable credentials say who vouches for the claims; "VCs do not replace GS1 identity or EPCIS 2.0 events, they layer trust on top of them" — [GovGDS (secondary, vendor)](https://govgds.com/verifiable-credentials-dpp/)
- GS1 has published work on combining VCs with EPCIS for end-to-end traceability (2022 draft white paper) and on digitalising product certificates and claims as credentials (2023). Status: white papers, not standards — [GS1 white paper on VCs and EPCIS](https://ref.gs1.org/docs/2022/GS1-WhitePaper-VerifiableCredentials-EPCIS-end-to-end-traceability); [GS1 certificates paper](https://www.gs1.org/sites/gs1/files/2023-04/digitalisation-of-product-certificates-claims-and-credentials.pdf)
- GS1 in Europe states that verifiable credentials would be an ideal technology to make supply-chain data trustworthy and notes JSON-LD is the VC payload format, which aligns with EPCIS 2.0 JSON-LD — [GS1 in Europe DPP paper](https://gs1.eu/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/GS1-Standards-Enabling-DPP.pdf)
- UNTP's Digital Traceability Event covers process events such as transformation, shipment or repair, and is the UN-level analogue of signed EPCIS events — [UNTP spec mirror, transparency graphs](https://spec-untp-fbb45f.opensource.unicc.org/docs/0.7.0/design-patterns/TrustGraphs/)
- Open-source implementer: OpenEPCIS documents signing passports so that they verify outside the issuer's own stack — [OpenEPCIS signed passports](https://openepcis.io/docs/digital-product-passport/signed-passports/)

### Inferences
- Mapping to passport content: commissioning (ObjectEvent, action ADD) creates the serial or batch and is the natural anchor for the DPP record; TransformationEvent links input batches to output batches and is how upstream claims (origin, recycled content, a raw-material certificate of analysis) can be inherited by a finished product; AggregationEvent gives the pack-case-pallet hierarchy; shipping and receiving ObjectEvents give chain of custody.
- For regulated goods the TransformationEvent chain is the critical and hardest part, because it exposes supplier relationships (see section 8).
- EPCIS repositories are usually private per company; a KYP layer needs either bilateral event sharing or signed event digests, not a shared event database.

### Gaps
- No source found showing that any JTC 24 standard or ESPR delegated act makes EPCIS normative for DPP event data.
- Normative field-level definitions of EPCIS event types were not read (only the ISO front matter was visible in search).
- No independent data on how many DPP platforms actually ingest EPCIS versus proprietary event feeds.

## 4. Verifiability: W3C VC 2.0, DIDs, UNTP, eIDAS 2.0 / EUDI / European Business Wallet, seals, vLEI

### Takeaway
Proving who issued a claim and that it is unaltered is solved at the cryptographic level (digitally signed credentials with a resolvable issuer identifier and a revocation list) and EN 18246 now accepts several such mechanisms for DPP data. What is not yet settled is the trust framework above the signature: which register vouches that an issuer identifier belongs to a real legal entity and that the entity is an accredited conformity assessment body. UNTP (close to but apparently not yet at version 1.0), the European Business Wallet (still in legislative procedure) and vLEI (standardised, thin adoption) are three competing or complementary answers.

### Cited Findings
- EN 18246 (PUBLISHED 16 September 2026, not yet cited in the Official Journal) is the DPP trust layer: passport data is electronically signed so origin and integrity can be checked. Accepted approaches listed by sources: W3C Verifiable Credentials, eIDAS electronic attestations of attributes, ISO 22376 Visible Digital Seal, or ISO/IEC 20248 digital signature structures, plus an audit record — [Trustrace (secondary)](https://trustrace.com/knowledge-hub/the-final-two-european-dpp-standards-are-here-what-it-means-for-fashion-and-apparel-brands); [DPPify (secondary)](https://www.dppify.com/blog/dpp-standards-en-18239-18246-published)
- None of the sources found say EN 18246 requires a qualified electronic seal specifically; eIDAS attestations are one option among several — [Blippa (secondary)](https://blippa.com/en/articles/dpp-standards-overview)
- W3C Verifiable Credentials Data Model 2.0 is a W3C Recommendation (2025). Status: PUBLISHED — [W3C VC Overview](https://www.w3.org/TR/vc-overview-1.0/); [Pyx commentary (secondary)](https://pulse.pyx.io/posts/250521VCstandard.html)
- Revocation: W3C Bitstring Status List v1.0 is published and a v1.1 exists; UNTP's VC profile requires Bitstring Status List for revocation and requires the status list credential to remain resolvable for as long as relying parties need to check it — [W3C Bitstring Status List v1.0](https://www.w3.org/TR/vc-bitstring-status-list/); [W3C v1.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/vc-bitstring-status-list-1.1/); [UNTP Verifiable Credentials profile](https://untp.unece.org/docs/specification/VerifiableCredentials/)
- UNTP status: version 0.7.0 was the public-review release; review has closed; the team stated it expected to release 1.0 by 1 September 2026 (the UN Bureau approval deadline), with about 50 public feedback issues being worked through in July 2026; a governance note says the 1.0 timeline was delayed. No official 1.0 release announcement was found. Status: late DRAFT (0.7) / 1.0 pending — [UNTP 0.7.0 specification](https://spec-untp-fbb45f.opensource.unicc.org/docs/0.7.0/specification/); [UNTP general updates](https://untp.unece.org/docs/governance/meetings/generalUpdates/); [UNTP Supply Chain Group notes](https://untp.unece.org/docs/governance/meetings/supplyChainGroup/)
- UNTP governance: UN/CEFACT (UNECE). A related UN/CEFACT project "Digital Product Passport Overview and Fundamental Principles" was in public review until 12 October 2026 — [GLEIF blog](https://www.gleif.org/en/newsroom/blog/building-trust-in-digital-trade-standards-are-moving-fast-identity-must-keep-up/)
- UNTP components: Digital Product Passport (issued by the shipper of goods; product identity, characteristics and sustainability performance for serialised items or batches; links to conformity credentials supporting its claims); Digital Conformity Credential (issued by conformity assessment bodies; independent verifiable assessment of products, facilities or organisations against published standards and regulations); Digital Facility Record (facility performance claims issued by the operator); Digital Traceability Event (transformation, shipment, repair); Digital Identity Anchor; Identity Resolver — [UNTP DPP specification](https://spec-untp-fbb45f.opensource.unicc.org/docs/specification/DigitalProductPassport); [UNTP transparency graphs](https://spec-untp-fbb45f.opensource.unicc.org/docs/0.7.0/design-patterns/TrustGraphs/)
- UNTP Digital Identity Anchor: a credential issued by a trusted authority (e.g. a business or tax register) asserting equivalence between a registered identity such as a VAT number and one or more DIDs. This is UNTP's answer to "who is behind this issuer key" — [UNTP Digital Identity Anchor](https://untp.unece.org/docs/specification/identity-anchor/)
- UNTP DID methods: did:web recommended for organisational identifiers; did:webvh where a register must retain control, because with did:web "the DID document is whatever the domain owner serves"; both resolve over DNS and HTTPS — [UNTP Digital Identity Anchor (spec mirror)](https://spec-untp-fbb45f.opensource.unicc.org/docs/specification/DigitalIdentityAnchor/)
- UNTP trust chain as specified: manufacturer DPP → third-party assessor Digital Conformity Credential → Digital Identity Anchor → trust anchor; graph verification flags any missing or invalid link for human review — [UNTP transparency graphs](https://spec-untp-fbb45f.opensource.unicc.org/docs/0.7.0/design-patterns/TrustGraphs/)
- Accreditation link is the weak spot today: the UNTP Conformity Group notes that evidence of an assessor's authority "today is typically a PDF or web link" and is expected to become a Digital Identity Anchor; a trust-register model for scheme endorsements was proposed in March 2026; the group is defining a best-practice trust-graph traversal profile — [UNTP Conformity Group notes](https://untp.unece.org/docs/governance/meetings/conformityGroup/); [UNTP Supply Chain Group notes](https://untp.unece.org/docs/governance/meetings/supplyChainGroup/)
- Revocation design decision (9 July 2026): UNTP will not adopt an enduring, status-rotating credential model and favours standalone credentials with revocation flags — [UNTP Conformity Group notes](https://untp.unece.org/docs/governance/meetings/conformityGroup/)
- UNTP adoption: pilots based on the Global Organic Textile Standard (GOTS) were used as the example for certification schemes; the site said a list of pilots and adoption pathways would be added later; implementer registrations exist as issues in the UNTP repository. No consolidated public implementer list was found — [UNTP general updates](https://untp.unece.org/docs/governance/meetings/generalUpdates/); [example implementer registration issue](https://opensource.unicc.org/un/unece/uncefact/spec-untp/-/issues/794)
- UNTP is referenced on an EU Commission transition-pathways page for textiles — [EU transition-pathways](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu/textiles/news/united-nations-transparency-protocol-untp)
- eIDAS 2.0 / EUDI Wallet: Member States must provide at least one EUDI Wallet by end of 2026; implementation for legal persons has lagged behind natural persons — [NTT DATA position paper (secondary)](https://dam.nttdata.com/api/public/content/European-Business-Wallet)
- European Business Wallet: Commission proposal of 19 November 2025 (Digital Package); builds on the EUDI framework with features for businesses and public bodies. Council position reached mid-2026 (sources give 22 May 2026 general approach and 9 June 2026 adoption); Parliament ITRE rapporteur draft reported April 2026. Status: DRAFT legislation, not in force — [European Parliament Legislative Observatory](https://oeil.europarl.europa.eu/oeil/en/document-summary?id=1866804); [Council document ST-5938-2026](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-5938-2026-INIT/en/pdf); [KPMG flash alert](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/our-insights/gms-flash-alert/2026/flash-alert-2026-151.html)
- Cost of the eIDAS route for small firms: EuroWindoor's feedback on the draft registry act cites roughly EUR 300 to 800 per year for qualified electronic seals or signatures and disproportionate security and training investment for small manufacturers — [EuroWindoor feedback, May 2026](https://www.eurowindoor.eu/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/EuroWindoor-Feedback-on-draft-Implementing-Act-on-DPP-Registry-2026-05.pdf)
- vLEI: standardised as ISO 17442-3 (published 2024), built on KERI and ACDC, with GLEIF as root of trust and Qualified vLEI Issuers under the vLEI Ecosystem Governance Framework. The active LEI population passed 3 million in Q1 2026. GLEIF positions the LEI/vLEI as the organisational identifier behind DPP data; this is GLEIF's own advocacy and no production DPP use of vLEI was found. Status: PUBLISHED standard, limited adoption — [GLEIF press release on ISO 17442-3](https://www.gleif.org/en/newsroom/press-releases/iso-standardizes-gleif-s-pioneering-digital-organizational-identity-offering-with-publication-of-vlei-technical-standard); [GLEIF vLEI page](https://www.gleif.org/en/organizational-identity/lei-vlei/the-verifiable-lei-vlei); [GLEIF blog](https://www.gleif.org/en/newsroom/blog/building-trust-in-digital-trade-standards-are-moving-fast-identity-must-keep-up/)
- Battery sector precedent for third-party attestation: the Battery Pass consortium published a dedicated paper on conformity assessment for battery passport data (2024), and TÜV SÜD with T-Systems ran a proof of concept for digitally signed, verifiable certificates of battery passport data via Catena-X — [Battery Pass conformity assessment paper](https://thebatterypass.eu/assets/images/content-guidance/pdf/2024_BatteryPassport_Conformity_Assessment.pdf); [Telekom Data Intelligence Hub on TÜV SÜD](https://dih.telekom.com/en/t-ue-v-sued-battery-pass-verification-via-catena-x)

### Inferences
- "KYC for products" decomposes into four checks, each with an existing building block: (a) issuer identity — is the signing key bound to a registered legal entity (eIDAS qualified seal certificate, EBW attestation, vLEI, or UNTP Digital Identity Anchor); (b) issuer authority — is that entity accredited for this scope (accreditation credential from a national accreditation body or scheme owner; today mostly a PDF or website lookup); (c) claim integrity and subject binding — signed credential whose subject is the product, batch or facility identifier, including for example a lab's certificate of analysis issued as a Digital Conformity Credential referencing the batch; (d) currency — status list check for revocation, suspension and expiry.
- Step (b) is the least digitised link everywhere. A KYP layer that maintains a machine-readable register of accredited labs and certification bodies and their scopes would fill a gap that UNTP itself acknowledges.
- Supporting more than one proof family is unavoidable: EN 18246 is multi-option, EU public-sector trust will lean on eIDAS seals and attestations, and global supply chains (non-EU suppliers who cannot easily obtain EU qualified certificates) will lean on W3C VC with did:web as in UNTP.
- did:web makes issuer identity only as strong as DNS and web hosting; for regulated goods it should be combined with an identity anchor or qualified certificate.

### Gaps
- Whether UNTP 1.0 has formally been released as of November 2026 could not be confirmed.
- No source found that specifies how the EUDI wallet or European Business Wallet will be used in the DPP system (e.g. for authenticating legitimate-interest users or sealing passports); the link is inferred from EN 18239 referencing eIDAS assurance levels.
- UNTP's selective-redaction mechanism and the detailed structure of the Digital Conformity Credential (accreditation fields) were not retrieved.
- No evidence of a production deployment where an accredited lab issues certificates of analysis as verifiable credentials at scale.
- Exact proof formats mandated by UNTP (Data Integrity vs. JOSE/COSE enveloping) were not confirmed in this session.

## 6. Blockchain: where used, where dropped, EBSI

### Takeaway
Blockchain is not required anywhere in the EU DPP stack, and the reference architecture, registry and standards work without it; trust comes from signatures and registers. It persists in specific niches (multi-party custody proofs, luxury and minerals provenance, EBSI's public-sector trust registries), and EBSI has moved from pilot to an operated service in 2026 but with DPP use still at pilot level.

### Cited Findings
- ESPR and the standards are technology-neutral; blockchain is not mandated — [dpp-tool (secondary)](https://dpp-tool.com/en/blog/dpp-blockchain/); [dppgrid (secondary)](https://dppgrid.com/resources/blockchain-product-passport)
- The registry adopted by the Commission is a conventional central index (identifiers, pointers) operated under Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1778 — [PassportCraft (secondary)](https://passportcraft.com/insights/eu-dpp-registry-who-must-register)
- EU Blockchain Observatory and Forum report "Digital Product Passports: A Blockchain-based Perspective": advises against using NFTs for DPPs because of conflicts with business confidentiality, and flags the absence of a trust framework and conformance programme for decentralised DPPs — [EUBOF DPP report](https://blockchain-observatory.ec.europa.eu/document/download/b6e3c85c-43c1-405b-aba8-e49a71249ef7_en?filename=EUBOF_DPP_report.pdf)
- Arguments against, from implementers: restricted passport data cannot be put on a public chain; for brand-authored data a chain adds no multi-party trust; data accuracy, supplier evidence and human review decide whether a passport works. Argument for: it earns its place only for tamper-evident proof of custody across mutually distrustful parties — [dppgrid (secondary)](https://dppgrid.com/resources/blockchain-product-passport); [myproductpassport.eu (secondary)](https://myproductpassport.eu/blog/blockchain-and-digital-product-passports-use-cases)
- EBSI: now operated by the EUROPEUM-EDIC consortium; an EBSI blog post of 22 October 2026 announces a business launch bringing trust services into production "as a service"; EBSI's own timeline says further services follow in 2027. Status: IN PRODUCTION for core trust services (recent), DPP use at PILOT level — [EBSI site](https://ebsi.eu/); [EBSI blog](https://ebsi.eu/blog); [ITdaily (secondary)](https://itdaily.com/news/cloud/ebsi-european-infrastructure/)
- EBSI's model: the ledger holds registries of trusted issuers and accreditation information so that the authority behind a credential can be verified; credentials themselves stay off-chain — [EBSI blog](https://ebsi.eu/blog); [EBSI supply chain page](https://ebsi.eu/industry/supply-chain)
- TRACE4EU battery materials pilot: EBSI Verifiable Credentials to authenticate battery supply chain data (product carbon footprint, third-party certifications, material origin), interoperable with Catena-X and UNECE. ### Inferences
- The defensible use of a ledger in a KYP layer is narrow: a public, append-only register of issuer keys, accreditation status and credential-status hashes (the EBSI pattern), or timestamp anchoring of event digests. Product data and supplier relations should stay off-chain.
- The same functions can be delivered by signed status lists and an HTTPS-hosted trust register, which is what UNTP does; the choice is governance (who runs the register) more than technology.

## 7. Anti-counterfeiting and the physical-digital link

### Takeaway
State of the art is layered: item-level serialisation with server-side scan analytics as the baseline, plus either a cryptographic NFC chip (dynamic message per tap, optional tamper loop) or a printed copy-detection pattern as the anti-cloning factor. Neither is absolute: chip authentication proves the chip is genuine, not the product inside, and copy-detection patterns have been shown clonable by machine-learning attacks under some conditions.

### Cited Findings
- The DPP baseline carrier is a plain smartphone-readable code (EN 18220), which by itself is copyable; the DPP standards address data authenticity (EN 18246), not physical authenticity — [idpp.net guide (secondary)](https://idpp.net/en/guides/dpp-standards-guide); [Trustrace (secondary)](https://trustrace.com/knowledge-hub/the-final-two-european-dpp-standards-are-here-what-it-means-for-fashion-and-apparel-brands)
- ISO 22376 Visible Digital Seal and ISO/IEC 20248 (digital signature data structures for barcodes and RFID) are among the EN 18246 options: they put a signed payload in the carrier itself, which proves the data is authentic offline but does not stop the code being copied — [DPPify (secondary)](https://www.dppify.com/blog/dpp-standards-en-18239-18246-published)
- NFC with cryptographic chips: NXP NTAG 424 DNA generates a fresh AES-based Secure Unique NFC (SUN) message with an incrementing counter on each tap, authenticated by an 8-byte truncated AES-CMAC, so a copied static URL fails verification. Basic NTAG 213/215/216 tags can be read and duplicated — [NFCore guide (vendor)](https://nfcore.app/guides/nfc-product-authentication-anti-counterfeiting); [NFCFYI chip reference (secondary)](https://nfcfyi.com/chip/ntag424/)
- Limits stated by the more careful sources: security depends on the back-end verifying the message and the counter history; residual replay risk must be assessed; chip authentication shows the chip is genuine, not that it is still attached to the original item; the TagTamper variant adds a loop that breaks when the tag is peeled or cut; factory overruns and diversion are outside what chip authentication proves; key personalisation and protected key storage are system requirements — [NFCore guide (vendor)](https://nfcore.app/guides/nfc-product-authentication-anti-counterfeiting); [Proudtek tamper tag (vendor)](https://proudtek.com/products/rfid-labels/ntag424-dna-tamper-evident-tag)
- Copy-detection patterns (secure QR): Chaban et al. (WIFS 2021) show that with a learned estimation model and an industrial printer, simple detection metrics "cannot reliably distinguish the original CDP from their fakes under certain printing conditions" — [arXiv 2110.02176](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02176)
- A May 2026 defence paper confirms the threat is current: machine-learning attacks can recover the digital template from a single high-quality scan and produce high-quality copies; it proposes an enrolment stage with a second camera to improve robustness, and references a commercial vendor's CDP lifecycle — [arXiv 2605.31292](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.31292)
- EU-level anti-counterfeiting infrastructure work exists on EBSI (EBSI-ELSA with EUIPO) — [EBSI-ELSA](https://ebsi.eu/projects/ebsi-elsa-euipo)
- Aggregation (pack to case to pallet) is expressed with EPCIS AggregationEvents, which is how serialised track-and-trace systems maintain the parent-child hierarchy — [iTeh ISO/IEC 19987:2024](https://standards.iteh.ai/catalog/standards/iso/d799a114-aba0-4b07-a3c4-45ff1edfb30f/iso-iec-19987-2024)

### Inferences
- Practical ranking for binding a record to a pack: (1) serialised 2D code plus scan-pattern analytics (cheap, detects mass copies after the fact, no proof at first scan); (2) add a copy-detection pattern (low unit cost, needs app or good camera, attackable by well-resourced counterfeiters); (3) cryptographic NFC with tamper loop (strongest consumer-verifiable option, higher unit cost, needs key management); (4) combine with tamper-evident closures so the authenticated element cannot be moved to a refilled or substitute pack.
- None of these covers genuine-pack diversion or factory overruns; those need commissioning controls (only authorised lines can commission serials) and aggregation/shipping events reconciled against scans.
- Because the DPP rules stop at data authenticity, physical authentication is an area where a KYP layer can differentiate without conflicting with the standards.

### Gaps
- Sources on NFC security were vendor and reseller material; no independent security evaluation or published attack on SUN implementations was found.
- No 2024-2026 independent benchmark of commercial copy-detection products was found.
- No reliable unit-cost figures were found for cryptographic NFC versus secure print at scale.
- Regulatory serialisation precedents (pharmaceutical and tobacco track-and-trace) were not researched in this session.

## 8. Privacy and confidentiality

### Takeaway
The EU design handles confidentiality mainly through access tiers (public versus restricted data, EN 18239) rather than cryptography. Cryptographic selective disclosure exists (SD-JWT in the EUDI stack, BBS at W3C candidate stage) but is immature for supply-chain use, and peer-reviewed work notes that no system yet combines zero-knowledge disclosure with EU DPP alignment; fear of exposing supplier relationships is a documented brake on data sharing.

### Cited Findings
- EN 18239 separates public fields from commercially confidential ones with role-based access and authentication aligned to eIDAS assurance levels — [Trustrace (secondary)](https://trustrace.com/knowledge-hub/the-final-two-european-dpp-standards-are-here-what-it-means-for-fashion-and-apparel-brands)
- DPP service providers may not sell, reuse or process passport data beyond what the service requires unless agreed with the economic operator — [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/digital-product-passports-in-the-eu-comprehensive-expansion)
- SD-JWT: issuer commits to salted hashes of claims; holder reveals chosen disclosures; verifier recomputes hashes. Limits: it reveals the number of claims, does not provide unlinkability and is not zero-knowledge — [TechRxiv survey on selective disclosure, Aug 2025](https://www.techrxiv.org/doi/pdf/10.36227/techrxiv.175492163.32399388/v1); [arXiv 2506.00262](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.00262)
- BBS: pairing-based signatures allowing derived proofs over selected messages with unlinkability; the W3C BBS cryptosuite is still a Candidate Recommendation; neither BBS nor SD-JWT (P-256) is quantum-safe. Status: DRAFT (CR) — [R. Broeckelmann analysis (secondary)](https://medium.com/@robert.broeckelmann/sd-jwt-vc-vs-bbs-2023-why-these-arent-really-the-things-you-re-comparing-438397847472)
- Academic state: a 2026 critical-minerals paper (OreProof) reports that stakeholders' willingness to share data hinges on privacy and disclosure concerns and that no peer-reviewed system unites hybrid architecture, zero-knowledge selective disclosure and EU DPP alignment for critical minerals — [arXiv 2609.00340](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.00340)
- Another research prototype combines Gaia-X alignment, blockchain anchoring and zero-knowledge proofs for vehicle passports — [arXiv 2509.06133](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.06133)
- EU Blockchain Observatory warns that public-ledger and NFT approaches conflict with business confidentiality — [EUBOF DPP report](https://blockchain-observatory.ec.europa.eu/document/download/b6e3c85c-43c1-405b-aba8-e49a71249ef7_en?filename=EUBOF_DPP_report.pdf)
- UNTP's architecture places third-party conformity credentials between the manufacturer's claim and the verifier, so a verifier can rely on an assessor's attestation rather than on raw supplier data — [UNTP transparency graphs](https://spec-untp-fbb45f.opensource.unicc.org/docs/0.7.0/design-patterns/TrustGraphs/)
- For NFC-based verification, what the verification website records about each tap is a design decision outside the chip's protections — [NFCore guide (vendor)](https://nfcore.app/guides/nfc-product-authentication-anti-counterfeiting)

### Inferences
- The workable pattern today for "prove the claim, hide the supplier" is attestation by a trusted third party: an accredited body inspects the confidential evidence and issues a credential about the product or batch that omits the supplier identity. This needs no advanced cryptography and fits UNTP's Digital Conformity Credential and the Battery Pass conformity-assessment approach.
- Cryptographic selective disclosure is useful at the edge (showing an authority more fields than the public) but should be an optional capability given standard immaturity.
- Scan data: each resolver hit can expose IP address, time, approximate location and a serialised item identifier; once linked to a purchaser or account this is personal data. A KYP resolver should minimise logging, separate authentication analytics from marketing use, and avoid item-level identifiers becoming persistent personal identifiers after sale. This is my reasoning from GDPR principles, not a sourced finding.

### Gaps
- No authoritative source (EDPB, EDPS, national authority or JTC 24 text) was found on GDPR treatment of DPP scan data or of item-level identifiers after sale.
- UNTP's own confidentiality design patterns (selective redaction, encrypted data behind the resolver) were not retrieved.
- No empirical evidence found on how often companies actually refuse to share upstream data for confidentiality reasons, beyond the qualitative statement in the hydrogen DPP study cited by OreProof.

## 9. Known hard problems and criticisms

### Takeaway
The dominant criticism is that the system validates presence and format of data, not truth: registry confirmation is explicitly not proof of compliance, and most firms lack the supplier data to populate passports. Cost and complexity for small firms, persistence after insolvency (addressed on paper by mandatory back-up but with the provider regime not yet legislated), and cross-provider interoperability across a multi-option standard set are the other recurring issues.

### Cited Findings
- Data quality at source: Euroconsumers argues the draft registry rules "establish a formal validation regime" that checks whether data is present, which is "insufficient to underpin a credible data quality system" — [Euroconsumers feedback, June 2026](https://www.euroconsumers.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Euroconsumers-Feedback-on-Commission-Draft-Implementing-Regulation-on-the-Digital-Product-Passport-Registry.pdf)
- The registry's registration identifier is not proof of compliance — [Regen Studio (secondary)](https://www.regenstudio.world/blog/dpp-registry-goes-live/)
- Supplier data gap: a Provenant survey reports 77% of businesses lack the supplier data needed for effective DPPs and only 23% collect comprehensive supplier data (vendor survey, self-reported) — [CFOtech UK](https://cfotech.co.uk/story/supplier-data-gap-threatens-digital-product-passports)
- A KPMG 2026 survey, as cited by a secondary guide, found 31% naming supplier and value-chain data collection as their main DPP challenge — [getcatalog.ai (secondary)](https://www.getcatalog.ai/glossary/digital-product-passport)
- SME cost: EuroWindoor cites about EUR 300 to 800 per year for qualified seals or signatures and disproportionate investment in security assessments, tools and training for small manufacturers on basic IT — [EuroWindoor feedback, May 2026](https://www.eurowindoor.eu/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/EuroWindoor-Feedback-on-draft-Implementing-Act-on-DPP-Registry-2026-05.pdf)
- Adoption brakes named by practitioners: regulatory ambiguity, high implementation cost, lack of standardised data infrastructure — [WIoT interview (secondary)](https://wiot-group.com/think/en/articles/jan-buchel-digital-product-passport-why-companies-not-ready/)
- Sector warning: an EU-funded textile report says DPP benefits such as textile-to-textile recycling may not materialise unless data quality and interoperability improve — [Apparel Resources (secondary)](https://apparelresources.com/business-news/sustainability/eu-digital-product-passports-at-risk-of-undermining-textile-circularity-report/)
- Persistence: ESPR requires the passport to remain available after insolvency, liquidation or cessation of the responsible operator, via a back-up copy held by an independent third-party service provider; EN 18221 specifies replication between operators and back-up operators and data lifetime rules — [Renoon (secondary)](https://www.renoon.com/blog/who-owns-the-data-in-a-digital-product-passport); [BSI on EN 18221](https://knowledge.bsigroup.com/products/digital-product-passport-data-storage-archiving-and-data-persistence)

### Inferences
- Interoperability risk is structural: EN 18219 allows several identifier schemes and EN 18246 several proof mechanisms, so two compliant providers can still fail to verify each other's passports without profiles. Expect sector profiles (as Battery Pass and Catena-X did) to do the real interoperability work.
- "Garbage in" is not solved by any published standard; the standards give provenance and integrity of statements, and the only available remedy for truth is third-party attestation with accountable, accredited issuers. This is the market opening for a KYP verification layer, and also its main cost driver.
- A new KYP layer inherits the persistence problem: credentials, status lists and trust registers must outlive both the product's lifetime and the KYP operator, so escrow or back-up of the verification data should be designed in from the start.
- Timing risk: with the first product delegated act expected late 2026 and at least 18 months' transition, mandatory ESPR passports for most goods arrive from 2028 onward; batteries (February 2027) are the first real test.
