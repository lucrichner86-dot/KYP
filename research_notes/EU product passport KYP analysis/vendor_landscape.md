# Vendor and market landscape: Digital Product Passport, provenance, traceability and product verification (as of November 2026)

Method note for the report writer: research was done on 13 Nov 2026 by web search only. Direct page fetches were blocked in this session (vendor hosts such as trustrace.com, renoon.com, dpp-tool.com, cannavigia.com, billions.network and hs.abiresearch.com are outside the session's allowed network domains), so every finding below rests on search-result extracts of the cited page, not on a full read. Items marked "vendor claim" are self-reported marketing. Funding figures from aggregators (CB Insights, Tracxn, PitchBook) conflict with each other and are flagged. Trademark registers (EUIPO, USPTO, Swissreg) could not be queried.

## 1. Dedicated DPP platforms and other archetypes: who sells what

### Takeaway
The market is crowded with 50+ vendors that publish and host passports, grouped into roughly eight archetypes; almost all of them publish data that the brand or its suppliers declare, and differentiation is on sector (fashion, batteries, industrial) and on data plumbing, not on independent verification. Gartner's first "Emerging Market Quadrant for DPP: Established Vendors" (6 July 2026) and ABI Research's ranking give the closest thing to a neutral shortlist, but both are paywalled and known only through vendor press releases.

## 2. Regulated goods: who serves them, and which serialization vendors are moving into DPP

### Takeaway
Regulated-goods traceability (pharma, tobacco, excise) is served by a separate, older set of serialization and government track-and-trace vendors (Antares Vision/rfxcel, Optel, SICPA, Inexto, Dentsu Tracking, Kezzler), and several now market DPP; cannabis in Europe is served by small vertical compliance vendors (Cannavigia, Ubirch/Cansativa, CannaTrust) with no mandated central system in Germany.

- US states typically mandate a single government-contracted platform (Metrc model); Metrc appears in the German lobby material as a reference — [GrowerIQ](https://groweriq.ca/2026/07/07/cannabis-track-and-trace-by-country-2026/)

### Inferences
- Serialization incumbents own the line-level integration and regulator relationships in pharma and tobacco; a KYP layer would more plausibly sit on top of their event data than replace it.
- European cannabis has no regulator-mandated product passport and no dominant verification brand; the existing offers are an ERP-style vendor passport (Cannavigia), a patient credential (Ubirch) and a private seal (CannaTrust).
- Cannabis is outside ESPR's first product groups, so demand for a cannabis KYP would come from GMP/GDP, pharmacy and importer due diligence, not from the DPP mandate itself.

### Gaps
- TraceLink: no DPP announcement found. SICPA: no DPP-specific product confirmed. BioTrack: nothing found for Europe.
- Scale, funding and customer counts for Cannavigia and CannaTrust were not found; whether CannaTrust is still operating in 2026 is unverified (source is an older press release).
- Status and likely design of any German federal cannabis track-and-trace system is unknown.

## 3. Independent verification: certification bodies, labs and trust-anchor positioning

### Takeaway
The large testing, inspection and certification (TIC) groups all have a DPP page, but what they sell is mainly advisory, readiness and supplier verification; DNV is the only one found with a branded verified-claims product. No company was found positioning as an independent "KYC for products" trust anchor, and the UN Transparency Protocol explicitly leaves the decision of whose attestations to trust to the verifier.

Trust.": blockchain-based product that issues a statement, certificate of conformity or product passport with a unique digital identity and a QR for checking origin and authenticity; DNV's DPP page stresses "verified data and third-party validation" — [DNV Tag. Trace. Trust.](https://dnv.com/se/services/tag-trace-trust--197430); [DNV DPP](https://www.dnv.com/services/digital-product-passport-dpp/)

- DNV Ventures is an investor in Kezzler — [Nordic9](https://nordic9.com/news/kezzler-secures-nok-57-million-from-investinor-and-dnv-ventures)
- SGS's DPP offer covers supplier verification, testing and DPP readiness; pages do not mention verifiable credentials or a digital verification product — [SGS](https://www.sgs.com/en/showcases/digital-product-passports)
- Intertek launched "comprehensive Digital Product Passport services" on 13 March 2026, described as an advisory suite for brands and manufacturers — [Intertek](https://w3inte.intertek-france.com/news/2026/intertek-launches-comprehensive-digital-product-passport-services/)
- TÜV Austria has ESPR and DPP service pages; Bureau Veritas offers traceability services — [TÜV Austria](https://be.tuvaustria.com/dpp-digital-product-passport/); [Bureau Veritas](https://www.cps.bureauveritas.com/needs/traceability-solutions-bureau-veritas)
- A TÜV Rheinland-related pitch deck describes a battery DPP in which a product identifier collects verifiable credentials from authorised entities (W3C DID, OpenID4VC) and lists "differentiation from SGS DPP" as an open question; authorship and status unclear — [The Circular Lab deck](https://radar.thecircularlab.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/1764490711.pdf)
- UNTP trust model: a manufacturer's DPP is backed by a Digital Conformity Credential issued by a conformity assessment body, whose identity is anchored by a Digital Identity Anchor from an authoritative register or accreditation authority; verifiers need only a short list of accreditation authorities as trust anchors, and UNTP "does not decide whose attestations a reader must accept" — [UNTP Digital Identity Anchor](https://untp.unece.org/docs/specification/DigitalIdentityAnchor); [UNTP trust graphs](https://spec-untp-fbb45f.opensource.unicc.org/docs/0.7.0/design-patterns/TrustGraphs/)
- Science-based origin verification exists as a separate niche: Oritain tests physical samples ("the physical truth of your products, not just their paperwork"); GenuTrace launched in May 2025 with isotope and elemental analysis — [Oritain](https://oritain.com/platform/platform-overview); [Textile World](https://www.textileworld.com/?p=104446)
- Provenance (London) is the closest commercial model of "verified claims as a product": Proof Points link brand claims to supply-chain data and third-party verification; self-reported 1,600+ brands and 81,000+ active Proof Points; retailers include Mecca and Cult Beauty; vendor claims uplift of up to 41% in add-to-cart — [Provenance](https://www.provenance.org/); [Mecca](https://mecca.com/en-au/m-pact/provenance)
- A Provenance-commissioned 2022 report found 85% of shoppers trust an independent verifier (vendor-commissioned) — [Provenance report](https://info.provenance.org/hubfs/Skin%20Deep%20Beauty%20Report%202022.pdf)
- Horizon Europe project COMPLIANCE4DPP started 1 June 2026 to build automated DPP compliance and trust tooling (Gaia-X involved) — [Gaia-X](https://gaia-x.eu/trust-verified-compliance4dpp-turns-product-data-compliance-into-a-business-opportunity/)

### Inferences
- TIC firms hold the accreditation that makes an attestation legally meaningful but have not productised machine-verifiable, per-batch credentials at scale; credential vendors have the technology but no accreditation. The space between the two is where a KYP layer could sit, either partnering with accredited labs or aggregating their credentials.
- The UNTP design means an independent verifier's value lies in its acceptance rules and reputation, since the protocol itself is neutral.
- Provenance shows that verified-claim badges sell on conversion uplift in beauty; nothing equivalent was found for safety-critical regulated goods.

### Gaps
- Bureau Veritas, TÜV SÜD, TÜV Rheinland, DEKRA and UL Solutions: no dated DPP verification product launch found; absence in search is not proof of absence.
- No search result showed any firm using "KYC for products" or "product KYC" as positioning; this is a negative finding from limited searches.
- Commercial terms of DNV's verified-claims products were not found.

## 4. Prospective "DPP service providers" under ESPR

### Takeaway
The certified DPP service-provider role does not yet exist in practice: the Commission's delegated act on service-provider requirements has slipped from an expected Q4 2025 to an indicative 2027, and no vendor announcement of intent to seek certification could be verified. The central EU DPP registry, by contrast, is live.

### Cited Findings
- ESPR defines a DPP service provider as an independent third party authorised by the economic operator, and requires a backup copy of the passport to be available through such a provider — [Circulor explainer](https://circulor.com/articles/eus-digital-product-passport-standards); [GS1 UK](https://www.gs1uk.org/insights/news/EU-Digital-Product-Passport-Registry-goes-live)
- The Commission launched the DPP Registry with a test environment and helpdesk on 20 July 2026 (one source says 19 July); the operating rules are in Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1778, adopted 16 July 2026, in force 6 August 2026 — [CMS](https://cms.law/en/int/legal-updates/key-development-in-the-eu-digital-product-passport-launch-of-the-dpp-registry); [GS1 in Europe](https://gs1.eu/news/european-commission-launches-the-digital-product-passport-registry/); [Sourcemap](https://www.sourcemap.com/blog/the-eu-central-dpp-registry-launches-on-july-19th---here-s-what-you-need-to-know)
- The registry is an index of identifiers and metadata; passport data stays decentralised with companies or their service providers — [GS1 UK](https://www.gs1uk.org/insights/news/EU-Digital-Product-Passport-Registry-goes-live)
- The delegated act on DPP service providers is listed on a Commission timeline for 2027 (Q2/Q3, indicative); an earlier law-firm note had expected Q4 2025 — [Renoon update](https://www.renoon.com/blog/digital-product-passport-latest-updates-the-next-developments-to-watch); [HLC](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/digital-product-passports-in-the-eu-comprehensive-expansion)
- A mid-2026 vendor blog states that the EU certification scheme for DPP service providers does not exist yet — [DPP-Tool](https://dpp-tool.com/en/blog/certified-dpp-provider/)
- DIGITALEUROPE asked the Commission in December 2024 to clarify what "independent" means for service providers — [DIGITALEUROPE](https://www.digitaleurope.org/resources/shaping-dpp-service-providers-building-a-secure-and-flexible-framework/)
- First ESPR product group is iron and steel, with a delegated act indicatively in 2026; product-specific requirements phase in from 2027 to 2030 — [Renoon update](https://www.renoon.com/blog/digital-product-passport-latest-updates-the-next-developments-to-watch); [Fraunhofer IML](https://www.iml.fraunhofer.de/en/projects/2026/digital-product-passport-eu.html)

### Inferences
- Any vendor calling itself a "certified DPP service provider" today is ahead of the law. Vendors listed on GS1 marketplaces as "DPP-as-a-Service" (todo GmbH, Data Graphs) and hosting-focused platforms are the obvious candidates once the scheme exists.
- The independence requirement could exclude vendors owned by, or tightly coupled to, the economic operator, which favours neutral third parties.

### Gaps
- No verified public statement by a named vendor that it will seek certification. The slipped timeline should be checked against the Commission's own ESPR working plan before being relied on.
- Content of the draft delegated act (capital, security, independence tests) was not retrieved.

- Publishing-only DPP is being commoditised at the low end; a verification product cannot be priced against EUR 9/month tools and needs a different value metric (per verified batch, per attestation, per accepted supplier).

### Gaps

## 7. "Know Your Product" / "KYP": existing meanings, users and brand implications

### Takeaway
"Know Your Product" is an established regulatory term in Canadian securities law and a familiar concept in US and EU investment regulation, and "KYP" is widely used in identity verification for "Know Your Patient" and by a UK risk firm for "Know Your Partner". In supply chains the phrase appears only in scattered blockchain projects (CircularPass/Billions, BlokTime); no dominant brand or confirmed trademark for product provenance was found, though registers were not checked.

### Cited Findings
Financial regulation
- Canada: the Client Focused Reforms added an express know-your-product rule in section 13.2.1 of NI 31-103; KYC, KYP and suitability reforms came into force on 31 December 2021; the final rule was narrowed from the 2018 proposal — [Fasken](https://www.fasken.com/en/knowledge/2019/10/final-amendments-to-national-instrument-31-103); [CSA FAQ, Dec 2023](https://www.securities-administrators.ca/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/CFRsFAQsDecember2023EN.pdf)
- CSA/CIRO Staff Notice 31-368 (10 December 2025) reviewed firms' KYC, KYP and suitability practices and gave further guidance — the term is in active regulatory use — [ASC](https://www.asc.ca/-/media/ASC-Documents-part-1/Regulatory-Instruments/2025/12/6255537-CSA-CIRO-Notice-31-368-Client-Focused-Reforms.pdf)
- US: FINRA Regulatory Notice 12-03 requires a reasonable investigation of any recommended security, with more scrutiny for complex products (the "reasonable-basis" limb of suitability); FINRA does not use KYP as a rule title — [FINRA RN 12-03](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/12-03)
- EU: MiFID II product governance (Articles 16(3) and 24(2)) obliges manufacturers and distributors to define a target market and understand the products they offer; ESMA guidelines applied from 3 January 2018 and were revised in 2023 — [Osborne Clarke](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/mifid-ii-a-practical-consideration-of-the-new-product-governance-requirements); [Arendt](https://www.arendt.com/jcms/p_131469/en/updated-esma-guidelines-on-mifid-ii-product-governance-requirements)

Supply chain and provenance uses
- CircularPass (Blue Room Innovation) on the "CircularTrust" blockchain with iden3: a Billions Network case study titled "Global KYP for sustainable compliance" uses KYP for product traceability in which each actor has a DID and each product event is signed (vendor-authored, no independent confirmation, pilot participants not named) — [Billions](https://billions.network/blog/global-kyp-for-sustainable-compliance-how-circularpass-proved-verifiable-supply-chains-are-ready)
- BlokTime documentation has a "Know Your Product (KYP)" section: a permissioned partner network exposing an item's chain from raw material to sale — [BlokTime docs](https://bloktime.gitbook.io/product-docs/bloktime/protocol-overview-and-summary/know-your-product-kyp)
- The adjacent, more common supply-chain phrase is "Know Your Supplier (KYS)", used by ImpactBuying and KYB vendors — [ImpactBuying](https://impactbuying.com/kys/); [The KYB](https://thekyb.com/knowledgebase/know-your-supplier-kys/)

Other "KYP" meanings
- "Know Your Patient" is the dominant KYP meaning among identity-verification vendors (Persona, Regula, Shufti, iDenfy, Ondato, KYC Hub, Bynn), including for online pharmacies — [Persona](https://withpersona.com/blog/know-your-patient-kyp-how-to-mitigate-healthcare-fraud); [Regula](https://regulaforensics.com/glossary/identity/know-your-patient-kyp/); [iDenfy](https://idenfy.com/blog/kyc-for-online-pharmacies/)
- "KYP" is the trading name of a UK third-party risk intelligence platform with the slogan "Know Your Partner" (merchant monitoring, supply-chain due diligence); an aggregator lists it as founded 2021 with about 91 staff (unverified) — [BounceWatch](https://bouncewatch.com/company/kyp)

### Inferences
- Opportunity: the KYC analogy is instantly legible to regulators, compliance officers and investors, and no supply-chain player owns the phrase. Exact-match domains (knowyourproduct.com, kyp.org) would be defensible assets where the term stays descriptive.
- Confusion risk 1: in cannabis and pharmacy, "KYP" already reads as Know Your Patient, the buyer-side identity check. A product-verification brand in that sector would sit beside patient-verification vendors using the same acronym.
- Confusion risk 2: search results for "know your product" in English are dominated by Canadian securities compliance, so organic discovery competes with law firms and regulators.
- Trademark risk: the phrase is descriptive for product-information services, which makes registration of the bare words harder and third-party descriptive use likely; a UK "KYP" firm already operates in supply-chain due diligence, a close class.
- Crypto association: the only supply-chain users of "KYP" found are blockchain projects, which may colour perception given the failures in section 6.

### Gaps
- EUIPO, USPTO, UKIPO and Swissreg were not queried; no statement can be made about existing registrations of "KNOW YOUR PRODUCT" or "KYP". This needs a proper clearance search.
- Current use and ownership of knowyourproduct.com and kyp.org were not checked.
- "Know Your Provider" in healthcare was not confirmed by any retrieved source.

## 8. Patterns, crowded areas and gaps for a KYP verification layer

### Takeaway
Publishing, hosting and supplier-data collection are crowded and commoditising; independent, machine-verifiable attestation of product claims for safety-regulated goods is the open space, with cannabis in Europe having neither a mandated system nor a dominant verifier.

### Cited Findings
- Crowded: fashion DPP, with ABI forecasting 62.5 billion apparel passports by 2030 and at least nine named vendors — [FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.in/news/business/over-62-5-billion-global-digital-product-passports-expected-to-be-created-by-2030/2024070545720)
- Crowded: low-cost passport publishing from EUR 9/month — [Net Zero Compare](https://netzerocompare.com/software/dpp-tool)
- Open: the standard trust model expects third-party conformity credentials but leaves acceptance to each verifier — [UNTP](https://untp.unece.org/docs/specification/DigitalIdentityAnchor)
- Open: the certified service-provider role is undefined until the delegated act, indicatively 2027 — [Renoon update](https://www.renoon.com/blog/digital-product-passport-latest-updates-the-next-developments-to-watch)
- Open: Germany has no central cannabis track-and-trace, while one is being lobbied for — [GrowerIQ](https://groweriq.ca/2026/07/07/cannabis-track-and-trace-by-country-2026/); [Lobbyregister](https://www.lobbyregister.bundestag.de/media/15/b3/843074/Lobbyregister-Regelungsvorhaben-Detailansicht-RV0021205-2026-09-17_16-41-46.pdf)
- Warning: prior provenance platforms failed on willingness to pay, not technology — [PierNext](https://piernext.portdebarcelona.cat/en/technology/the-closure-of-tradelens/)
- Precedent: serialization incumbents resell credential specialists instead of building (Antares Vision reselling Spherity) — [WhatTheyThink](https://whattheythink.com/news/128567-antares-vision-group-spherity-expand-partnership-reseller-agreement-deliver-dscsa-atp-credentials/)

### Inferences
- Pattern 1: data entry is overwhelmingly self-declared (supplier portals, ERP/PLM/PIM feeds); "verified" in vendor copy usually means document upload or a signature on the declarant's own data.
- Pattern 2: verification, where present, is one of three kinds: signature integrity (Spherity, Veribl, Arianee), accredited attestation (DNV, labs) or physical forensics (Oritain). No vendor found combines all three for one regulated category.
- Pattern 3: channel partnerships dominate (label makers, serialization vendors, PIMs, GS1 marketplaces), so a verification layer could distribute through existing passport vendors as a credential source instead of competing for the passport itself.
- Gap for KYP: per-batch lab results (CoA), GMP/GDP certificates and licence status issued as verifiable credentials by accredited parties, with a neutral acceptance policy, for cannabis first. Likely competitors are Cannavigia extending Product Pass, a TIC firm productising credentials, or a serialization vendor adding a cannabis module.
- Risk: without a mandate, payers must be found among importers, wholesalers and pharmacies with due-diligence liability; consumer-scan value alone did not sustain earlier ventures.

### Gaps
- No evidence was gathered on buyer willingness to pay for cannabis product verification in Europe.
- Whether labs serving the European cannabis market already issue digitally signed CoAs was not established beyond vendor statements.
