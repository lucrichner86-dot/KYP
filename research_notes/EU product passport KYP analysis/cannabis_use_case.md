## 1. Legal status and market structure in Europe as of Nov 2026

### Takeaway
Germany is by far the largest market (medical flower imports of 67.6 t in Q1 2026 alone, ministry forecast about 300 t for 2026) and runs on pharmacy-dispensed prescription medicine plus non-commercial clubs; commercial adult-use ("pillar 2") is effectively dead, and the MedCanG tightening (in-person prescription, mail-order ban) was still not adopted as of autumn 2026. Switzerland is the only European market where track-and-trace of adult-use product is mandatory and already run on Cannavigia, but full legalisation (CanPG) was sent back to a sub-commission in May 2026 and has no confirmed timetable.

### Inferences
- In Germany the addressable product today is a **prescription medicine dispensed by pharmacies**, which makes pharmaceutical law and HWG the binding constraint for any consumer-facing layer. Clubs are small (about 3.5% of supply) and pillar-2 retail does not exist.
- The German market's volume depends heavily on telemedicine plus mail-order. If the MedCanG amendment passes, volume shifts to local pharmacies and in-person doctors, which may raise the value of independent product information at the pharmacy counter but shrinks the online platforms that currently present product data.
- Switzerland is the natural home market: mandated track-and-trace already exists and Cannavigia holds the data, but the user base is small (about 10,500 participants) and the national law is years away.

## 2. What traceability and labelling each regime requires; ESPR/DPP scope

### Takeaway
No European regime requires a consumer-facing digital record. Germany's clubs must hand over a paper information slip with six data points and keep five years of records; Swiss pilots require a sealed pack with neutral product information, THC/CBD in percent and a reference to the trial, under self-control with accredited labs; German medical flower follows Ph. Eur. monograph 3028 and pharmacy compounding labelling. Medicinal products, food and feed are exempt from the ESPR, so medical cannabis has no DPP obligation; hemp textiles fall under the first ESPR priority group.

### Cited Findings

**Germany: KCanG clubs**
- § 21 KCanG: at hand-over the association must give an information slip with at least **weight, harvest date, best-before date, variety, average THC content and average CBD content in percent**. — [§ 21 KCanG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/kcang/__21.html); [LAGeSo Berlin, Merkblatt Anlage C](https://www.berlin.de/lageso/verbraucherschutz/konsumcannabis/kcang_merkblatt_anlage_c.pdf)
- Berlin's authority additionally **recommends a batch number** so products can be traced in complaints or recalls (recommendation, not statute). — [LAGeSo Berlin](https://www.berlin.de/lageso/verbraucherschutz/konsumcannabis/kcang_merkblatt_anlage_c.pdf)
- § 26 KCanG: stock-related documentation broken down by variety and average THC/CBD content; **five-year retention**; annual reporting to the authority. — [§ 26 KCanG (rewis)](https://rewis.io/gesetze/kcang/p/26-kcang/); [LSJV Rheinland-Pfalz Merkblatt](https://lsjv.rlp.de/fileadmin/lsjv/Themen/Gesundheit/Suchtpraevention/Cannabis/Merkblatt_Entsorgung_Cannabis_Vermehrungsmaterial_2025.pdf)
- In 2024 the Bundesrat asked the federal government to issue the ordinance under § 21(4) KCanG that could add labelling duties to the slip. — [Bundesrat Drs. 490/24(B)](https://dserver.bundestag.de/brd/2024/0490-24B.pdf)
- State authorities (e.g. Bavaria LGL, Brandenburg LAVG) supervise the associations. — [LGL Bayern](https://www.lgl.bayern.de/produkte/ueberwachung/konsumcannabisgesetz.htm); [LAVG Brandenburg](https://lavg.brandenburg.de/lavg/de/verbraucherschutz/anbauvereinigungen-konsumcannabis/)

**Germany: medical flower**
- Ph. Eur. monograph "Cannabis flower" (3028, 07/2024) applies from 1 July 2024 and replaces the national monographs of NL, DK, DE and CH; one portal gives 1 April 2025 as the date it became binding via the national edition. — [ALS](https://www.alsglobal.com/de/News-and-publications/2024/03/Neue_einheitliche_Cannabisblueten); [Deutsches Cannabis Portal](https://deutschescannabisportal.de/2023/08/30/bestellung-lagerung-pruefung-und-vernichtung/)
- Monograph types: THC-dominant = total THC minimum 5.0% (dried drug), total CBD maximum 1.0%; intermediate and CBD-dominant types defined separately. — [Ph. Eur. 3028 text copy](https://dl.novachem.com.au/files/Ph%20Eur%203028%20Cannabis%20flos.pdf)
- Pharmacies receive flower as a starting material, perform identity testing, then portion and label it as a compounded preparation (Rezeptur). — [Pharmazeutische Zeitung](https://www.pharmazeutische-zeitung.de/cannabis-in-der-apotheke-119427/); [MedCanOneStop](https://medcanonestop.com/blog/allgemein/wie-medizinisches-cannabis-in-apotheken-geprueft-wird/)
- Irradiation: a pharmacy site states manufacturers must say on the leaflet or the certificate of analysis whether the product was irradiated (not a legal text). The DPhG pharmaceutical-biology section has published a position on the microbiological quality of cannabis flower. — [Löns-Apotheke](https://www.loens-apotheke.de/medizinalcannabis/bestrahlungvoncannabis); [DPhG](https://www.dphg.de/artikel/stellungnahme-der-dphg-fachgruppe-pharmazeutische-biologie-zum-thema-medizinischer-cannabis-zur-mikrobiologischen-qualitat-von-cannabisbluten)
- GMP scope is under pressure: the Hessian authority (HLfGP) takes the view that GMP must apply from harvest and that the Qualified Person releasing each batch is responsible; BfArM has clarified cultivar approval as EU-GMP scrutiny increases. — [StratCann](https://stratcann.com/news/germanys-bfarm-clarifies-cultivar-approval-as-eu-gmp-scrutiny-increases/); [ECA Academy](https://www.gmp-compliance.org/gmp-news/what-are-the-gmp-requirements-for-medical-cannabis)

**Switzerland: pilot trials (BetmPV, version of 15 May 2021)**
- Art. 9 product quality: total THC max **20%**; oral products max **10 mg total THC per unit**. — [BetmPV PDF on bag.admin.ch](https://www.bag.admin.ch/dam/bag/de/dokumente/npp/cannabis/pilotversuche/verordnung-ueber-pilotversuche-nach-dem-betaeubungsmittelgesetz.pdf.download.pdf/fedlex-data-admin-ch-eli-cc-2021-217-20210515-de-pdf-a-1.pdf)
- Packaging: sealed pack only; child-resistant for oral products; pack must carry **neutral product information, weight, declaration of ingredients incl. total THC and total CBD in percent, reference to the specific pilot trial** (a health warning appears in the draft text; presence in the final text not confirmed here). — [BetmPV PDF](https://www.bag.admin.ch/dam/bag/de/dokumente/npp/cannabis/pilotversuche/verordnung-ueber-pilotversuche-nach-dem-betaeubungsmittelgesetz.pdf.download.pdf/fedlex-data-admin-ch-eli-cc-2021-217-20210515-de-pdf-a-1.pdf); [draft copy](https://projects.piratenpartei.ch/attachments/download/2746/BetmG_Pilotversuche_V_de.pdf)
- Producers determine THC/CBD content themselves and have batch samples analysed by certified labs; compliance with cultivation/product quality, packaging and product information rests on **self-control (Art. 30 BetmPV)**. — [BAG, exemption permits page](https://www.bag.admin.ch/de/ausnahmebewilligungen-im-zusammenhang-mit-pilotversuchen-nach-artikel-8a-betmg)
- The BAG publishes a health-protection orientation framework for the pilots. — [BAG Orientierungsrahmen](https://www.bag.admin.ch/dam/bag/de/dokumente/npp/cannabis/pilotversuche/gesundheitsschutzkonzept.pdf.download.pdf/Gesundheitsschutz,%20Jugendschutz%20und%20Pr%C3%A4vention_Orientierungsrahmen%20f%C3%BCr%20die%20Pilotversuche%20mit%20Cannabis.pdf)

**Netherlands**
- Coffee shops in the experiment must report track-and-trace data from receipt of delivery to point of sale; product testing is said to cover THC, CBD, heavy metals, pesticides, micro-organisms and aflatoxins (vendor guide, not an official source). — [GrowerIQ](https://groweriq.ca/netherlands-cannabis-track-trace-requirements/)

**ESPR / Digital Product Passport**
- The Commission states the ESPR covers virtually all physical products with a few exemptions "for example, for food and feed, and medicinal products". — [European Commission ESPR page](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/strategy/circular-economy/ecodesign-sustainable-products-regulation_en)
- First ESPR working plan (COM(2025) 187, adopted 16 April 2025): priority final products are textiles/apparel, furniture, tyres, mattresses; intermediates iron/steel and aluminium; horizontal measures on repairability and recycled content; mid-term review 2028. — [One Click LCA](https://oneclicklca.com/en-gb/resources/articles/first-espr-working-plan); [QIMA](https://blog.qima.com/sustainability/ecodesign-regulation-workplan)
- No source found addresses cannabis or hemp under the ESPR explicitly. — [European Commission ESPR page](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/strategy/circular-economy/ecodesign-sustainable-products-regulation_en)

### Inferences
- Medical cannabis (a medicinal product) and edible/CBD food products are outside the ESPR, so a DPP for cannabis would be **voluntary**. Hemp textiles would be caught by the textile delegated act. Non-medical adult-use flower (Swiss pilots, German clubs) is neither a medicinal product nor a food in the usual sense; nothing found says it is excluded, but it is on no working-plan list, so no DPP duty is foreseeable before 2030.
- The KCanG slip is the only place in German law where harvest date and best-before date must reach the end user. Medical patients, who pay more, are not legally entitled to the same fields in a standard form.
- Swiss pilots rely on self-control plus a mandated tracking system. The verification gap (who checks the producer's own THC figure) is structural.

### Gaps
- Exact text of KCanG §§ 6, 17, 18, 21, 26 and the current BetmPV could not be opened; verify wording, and whether a § 21(4) ordinance has since been issued.
- The 90–110% declared-content tolerance for medical flower and the legal basis for irradiation labelling (AMRadV) were not confirmed by any source in this session (background, unsourced: the former DAB monograph set ±10% of the declared content).
- No authoritative legal analysis of adult-use cannabis or CBD cosmetics under the ESPR found. CBD cosmetics fall under the Cosmetics Regulation (background, unsourced).
- GDP/GACP requirements per country not individually sourced.

## 3. North America and elsewhere as precedents

### Takeaway
The clearest precedent is Metrc Retail ID: an item-level QR issued through the state tracking system, mandatory in New York since March 2026 at $0.10 per code and voluntary in California (about 20% of weekly volume), with consumer recall lookup. Canada tracks at federal level and requires excise stamps but explicitly gives QR codes no regulatory status; Thailand moved to prescription-only with GACP farm traceability in June 2025.

### Cited Findings
- Metrc Retail ID launched in California in Nov 2024; about **20% of California's weekly product volume** carries a Retail ID QR; run rate of 1 million QR codes per week reported. — [Cannabis Equipment News](https://www.cannabisequipmentnews.com/processing/news/22943414/metrc-retail-id-achieves-1-million-weekly-run-rate-of-qr-codes); [Meadow](https://getmeadow.com/blog/metrc-retail-id)
- Aug 2025: Metrc added a direct-to-consumer recall feature in California; scanning the Retail ID QR shows whether the item is recalled. — [Metrc](https://www.metrc.com/news/direct-to-consumer-product-recall/); [Cannabis Science and Technology](https://www.cannabissciencetech.com/view/new-metrc-feature-lets-california-cannabis-consumers-scan-for-recalls)
- New York: distributors could apply Retail Item IDs from 1 Feb 2026; from **1 March 2026 every unit received by dispensaries must carry a Retail Item ID QR**; after 31 March 2026 units without the QR and a "TestPassed"/"RetestPassed" status in Metrc are out of compliance. Licensees pay **$0.10 per code**. — [NY OCM STS Bulletin 13](https://cannabis.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2026/01/sts-bulletin-13.pdf); [STS Bulletin 14](https://cannabis.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2026/02/sts-bulletin-14.pdf); [STS Bulletin 16](https://cannabis.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2026/03/sts-bulletin-16.pdf); [Distru summary](https://www.distru.com/metrc-bulletins/ny-metrc-retail-item-id-deadlines-for-2026)
- Massachusetts: Retail Item IDs optional; Massachusetts renewed its Metrc contract; Illinois licensees have access. — [Flourish](https://www.flourishsoftware.com/blog/a-comprehensive-guide-to-metrc-tags-and-retail-ids-for-cannabis-operators); [MJBizDaily](https://mjbizdaily.com/?p=401379); [GrowerIQ](https://groweriq.ca/illinois-cannabis-metrc-compliance/)
- Criticism: an industry piece calls out "the problems and hollow promises" of Retail ID (title only retrieved; competitor-adjacent source). — [Higher Origins](https://higherorigins.com/articles/the-problems-and-hollow-promises-of-metrcs-retail-id)
- Canada: the Cannabis Tracking and Licensing System tracks movement from cultivation to processing to sale (a reporting database, not a label element). An excise stamp is required on all legal products except those with under 0.3% THC; the stamp cannot be used to meet any other packaging or labelling requirement. — [Canada.ca, industry page](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/health/campaigns/cannabis/industry.html); [CRA EDM6-1](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/technical-information/excise-duty/excise-duty-memoranda/edm6-1-general-information-possession-sale-distribution-cannabis-products-cannabis-stamping-regime.html)
- Canada, 2025 streamlining amendments (reported as SOR/2025-43, in force 12 March 2025): QR codes permitted on labels (up to two barcodes), but a QR code does not exempt a product from any label requirement. — [Canada Gazette, proposal](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2024/2024-06-08/html/reg2-eng.html); [cannabisregulations.ai](https://www.cannabisregulations.ai/cannabis-and-hemp-regulations-compliance-ai-blog/canada-2026-packaging-label-flexibilities-qr-codes-compliance)
- California: AB 1965 requires dispensaries to provide the COA for any product on request from 1 Jan 2027; DCC's flower label checklist lists UID, licensee contact and packaging date but no QR. — [thisiswhyimhigh](https://thisiswhyimhigh.com/learn/california-right-to-request-a-coa) (consumer blog); [4 CCR § 17407](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/4-CCR-17407)
- Florida (hemp extract): the label QR must link directly to the COA or to a portal where the batch number retrieves it. — [ACS Laboratory](https://www.acslab.com/extraction/extraction-floridas-hemp-cbd-extract-labeling-rules-explained) (lab blog, possibly dated)
- Thailand: Notification on Controlled Herbs (Cannabis), 24–26 June 2025: flower only against a prescription (form PT 33) from a Thai-licensed practitioner, max 30 days' supply, single use; flower sold or exported must be traceable to a DTAM-certified GACP farm. — [High Times](https://hightimes.com/news/thailand-ends-recreational-cannabis-era-prescription-only-rule-takes-hold-amid-political-ouster/); [Deemed Consulting](https://www.deemedconsulting.com/insights/2026-05-12-thailand-june-2025-cannabis-regulation-exporters-cultivators); [Cannavigia blog](https://www.cannavigia.com/blog-posts/thailands-medical-only-cannabis-shift-what-it-means-for-cultivators-and-dispensaries-in-2025)

### Inferences
- New York shows a regulator can mandate an item-level consumer QR and pass the cost to licensees at about 10 US cents per unit. That is the only hard price point found for regulator-mandated consumer traceability.
- In both Canada and New York the consumer-facing code is tied to the official tracking system, not to an independent third party. An independent layer would have to add something the state system does not (cross-border chain, lab verification, cultivation practice).
- Thailand's GACP-farm traceability rule for exports is relevant because Thai flower enters the German medical chain; Cannavigia is already marketing to that segment.

### Gaps
- BioTrack and Leaf Data Systems failures (Washington 2018), Australia and Israel were not researched in this session (background, unsourced: Washington's Leaf Data rollout failed repeatedly and was replaced).
- What exact fields a Retail ID scan shows consumers (COA, licence, batch) was not retrieved from a primary source.
- Claims that California requires a COA-linked QR on labels appear only in commercial blogs and conflict with the DCC checklist description.

## 4. Known integrity problems a verification layer would address

### Takeaway
Potency inflation is the best-documented failure: in Colorado only 56.7% of flower products tested within ±15% of the labelled THC, and inter-lab variation on identical samples reached 38%; several US labs lost licences for inflating potency or passing contaminated product. In Germany the documented issues are batch recalls (mould suspicion, deviating THC, shortened shelf life) and "GMP washing" of imported flower, including one case of batches imported under a certificate later found non-compliant.

### Cited Findings
- University of Colorado study (Scientific Reports, July 2025): 277 products from 52 dispensaries; "accurate" = within 15% of label; **56.7% of flower versus 96% of concentrates** accurate; observed THC significantly lower than labelled. Funded by Colorado's Institute of Cannabis Research; testing by MedPharm, which also manufactures cannabis. — [CU Boulder](https://www.colorado.edu/today/2025/07/03/can-weed-labels-be-trusted-study-shows-it-depends-what-youre-buying); [MJBizDaily](https://mjbizdaily.com/thc-potency-inflated-in-most-colorado-marijuana-flower/); [CPR](https://www.cpr.org/2025/07/07/marijuana-potency-inaccurate-labels-cu-study/)
- Separate study (reported Nov 2025): identical samples sent to Colorado labs returned values varying by **up to 38%**, more than twice what state law allows. — [MJBizDaily](https://mjbizdaily.com/?p=407790)
- PLOS One study (Colorado School of Public Health/Stanford, reported April 2026): 74 Colorado products; packages reported higher THC than measured. — [NORML](https://norml.org/news/2026/04/23/study-labels-often-inflate-thc-potency-of-cannabis-products/)
- Earlier work found over 70% of products outside a 20% accuracy threshold, nearly all inflated; a peer-reviewed analysis of Nevada and Washington data examined bunching around the 20% THC threshold. — [ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2025/10/251023031618.htm) (attribution among returned links uncertain); [PMC7958443](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7958443)
- California enforcement: one lab's licence revoked (reported July 2024) for faking results and tampering with equipment so product potentially contaminated with pesticides and mould could pass; another lab denied renewal after THC was inflated on all ten randomly re-tested products; Verity Analytics fined then suspended for 12 violations with potency overstated by up to 32%; BelCosta Labs suspended (labelled 24.5% THC versus 17.4% and 14.7% on state re-test; company disputes); ProForma Labs lost its licence; California Ag Labs suspended two months. A fourth lab had lost its licence by the time of MJBiz's count. — [MJBizDaily, licence revoked](https://mjbizdaily.com/california-regulators-revoke-marijuana-testing-lab-license-for-faking-results/); [MJBizDaily, BelCosta](https://mjbizdaily.com/california-shutters-marijuana-testing-lab-belcosta-for-alleged-violations/); [MJBizDaily, fourth lab](https://mjbizdaily.com/news/fourth-california-cannabis-testing-lab-loses-license-as-crackdown-continues/395730/)
- Nevada: a Sparks lab was suspended 30 days and fined $70,000 for inflating THC by as much as 10%; Las Vegas lab Cannex faces loss of licence and a near-10-year ban (Nov 2025). — [Las Vegas Review-Journal](https://www.reviewjournal.com/news/politics-and-government/nevada/lab-boosted-thc-amounts-in-marijuana-tests-settlement-says-1957004/); [HighThere](https://highthere.com/news/shady-thc-results-possible-10-year-ban-nevada-cannabis-lab)
- Germany recalls: Mediprocan recalled "African Ruby" batch 2500101 in June 2025 on suspicion of mould; Tilray recalled a batch of extract THC5:CBD20 for deviating THC content (Sept 2026); Pedanios 22/1 batch E211 received a shortened shelf life after stability testing; a Cannamedical batch was withdrawn for unspecified impurities (date not established). — [apotheke adhoc, mould](https://www.apotheke-adhoc.de/nachrichten/detail/pharmazie/cannabis-rueckruf-wegen-schimmelverdacht/); [apotheke adhoc, Tilray](https://www.apotheke-adhoc.de/rubriken/detail/medizinisches-cannabis/abweichender-thc-gehalt-bei-tilray); [apotheke adhoc, shelf life](https://www.apotheke-adhoc.de/nachrichten/detail/pharmazie/verkuerzte-haltbarkeit-bei-cannabis-blueten/); [Soft Secrets](https://softsecrets.com/de/artikel/cannabismedizin-rueckruf-einer-charge)
- "GMP washing": flower grown under basic agricultural standards is passed through an EU-GMP facility to obtain a certificate; market sources allege uncertified product reached Germany via Central/Latin America through Canada (allegation, not an enforcement finding). — [MFLRC](https://mflrc.com/article/gmp-washing-germany-cannabis-drying-rule); [cannabis-startups.com](https://cannabis-startups.com/you-sell-gacp-flower-into-germany-and-call-it-gmp-that-might-be-over-soon-8-steps-you-need-to-take-now/)
- Mera Cannabis: after a July 2025 inspection, inspectors found non-compliance with GMP; several batches had been imported into the EU since the certificate was first issued. — [StratCann](https://stratcann.com/news/germanys-bfarm-clarifies-cultivar-approval-as-eu-gmp-scrutiny-increases/)
- A COA "reflects the sample tested, not the batch, and not the process that produced it" (industry source). — [Lumino Recruit](https://luminorecruit.com/cannabis-quality-assurance-eu-gmp-gacp-and-laboratory-testing/)
- Dutch experiment: early track-and-trace problems and a 500 g stock limit judged impractical (raised to a week's supply). — [Vicente LLP](https://vicentellp.com/insights/the-dutch-experiment-navigating-the-transition-to-a-regulated-cannabis-market-in-the-netherlands)

### Inferences
- The strongest quantified problem (THC inflation) is North American. For Germany no comparable independent label-accuracy study turned up; the German evidence is recalls and supply-chain certification doubts. A founder should not assume German patients face the same magnitude of mislabelling without data.
- The integrity gap in Europe sits **upstream of the pharmacy**: where the flower was actually grown and dried, under which standard, and whether the GMP certificate covers those steps. That is provenance data a track-and-trace vendor holds and a COA does not.
- A verification layer that only republishes the producer's own COA inherits the lab-shopping problem; value depends on who attests the data.

### Gaps
- No sources found on counterfeit legal packaging, forged COAs or diversion in Europe; no quantified data on illicit product in German pharmacies or clubs.
- No Swiss or Canadian recall statistics retrieved; no New York or Massachusetts lab enforcement retrieved.
- No German independent study of labelled versus measured THC/terpenes found.
- Irradiation as a quality complaint: only general pharmacy explanation found, no complaint data.

## 6. Advertising and information constraints

### Takeaway
In Germany, § 10(1) HWG bans advertising prescription-only medicines to the public, and the BGH on 26 March 2026 (I ZR 74/25, Wettbewerbszentrale v. Bloomwell/Algea Care) applied it to a cannabis platform even without naming a brand. Purely informational content without promotional intent remains permissible under the ECJ's MSD ruling (C-316/09), judged by overall presentation. KCanG § 6 bans all advertising and sponsorship for cannabis and for cultivation associations.

### Cited Findings
- § 10(1) HWG: prescription-only medicines may be advertised only to doctors, dentists, vets, pharmacists and persons lawfully trading in them. — [HWG text](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/heilmwerbg/HWG.pdf); [omsels.info commentary](https://www.omsels.info/die-verbote-oder-was-darf-ich-nicht/2-heilmittelwerbegesetz/10-hwg-verschreibungspflichtige-arzneimittel/10-abs-1-hwg)
- The BGH limits the ban to product-related advertising (product and sales promotion). — [omsels.info](https://www.omsels.info/die-verbote-oder-was-darf-ich-nicht/2-heilmittelwerbegesetz/bproduktbezogene-werbung)
- BGH, judgment of 26 March 2026, I ZR 74/25: the operator of an internet platform brokering treatments with prescription cannabis medicines breaches § 10(1) HWG when, by reference to complaints treatable with medical cannabis, it enables treatment requests to cooperating doctors. Advertising for a whole class of prescription medicines can be medicine advertising; absence of a named brand is no defence. — [NWB database entry](https://datenbank.nwb.de/Dokument/1091112/); [Ärzte Zeitung](https://www.aerztezeitung.de/Wirtschaft/BGH-untersagt-Werbung-fuer-aerztliche-Behandlungen-mit-medizinischem-Cannabis-462491.html); [itmr-legal](https://itmr-legal.de/blog/werbung-medizinisches-cannabis-bgh)
- Commentators: the ruling targets promotion, does not question medical use and does not by itself stop telemedicine. — [Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/de/insights-and-events/insights/2026/03/aktuelle-bgh-entscheidungen-telemedizin-im-fokus); [Strafverteidigung Dornbach](https://www.strafverteidigung-dornbach.de/rechtsbeitraege/werbung-fuer-medizinisches-cannabis-bgh-begrenzt-internetportale/)
- EU-law angle unsettled: one source says the BGH refused an ECJ referral and a constitutional complaint is pending; another says an EU freedom-of-services question has been referred. The separate Wellster case (I ZR 118/24, telemedicine with Irish doctors) is pending and may be referred. — [cannarzt](https://cannarzt.com/news/bgh-werbeverbot-cannabis-plattform/); [Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/de/insights-and-events/insights/2026/03/aktuelle-bgh-entscheidungen-telemedizin-im-fokus)
- ECJ C-316/09 (MSD Sharp & Dohme v Merckle, 5 May 2011): not every piece of information on a prescription medicine is advertising; advertising must be distinguished from purely informational statements without promotional intent; the manufacturer's economic interest alone is not enough; what counts is whether conduct, initiative and approach show an intention to promote prescription, supply or consumption, judged on the overall appearance. — [ECJ judgment](https://infocuria.curia.europa.eu/tabs/redirect/juris/document/document.jsf;jsessionid=9ea7d0f130d5f4987880ae24432a8a0bcc40d64b0d04.e34KaxiLc3eQc40LaxqMbN4PaNeMe0?text=&docid=79732&pageIndex=0&doclang=DE&mode=lst&dir=&occ=first&part=1&cid=2343484); [it-recht-kanzlei](https://www.it-recht-kanzlei.de/infos-verschreibungspflchtige-medikamente-werbung-internet.html)
- KCanG § 6: advertising and any form of sponsoring for cannabis and for cultivation associations is prohibited. — [KCanG text](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/kcang/KCanG.pdf)
- Swiss pilots require "neutral" product information on the pack. — [BetmPV PDF](https://www.bag.admin.ch/dam/bag/de/dokumente/npp/cannabis/pilotversuche/verordnung-ueber-pilotversuche-nach-dem-betaeubungsmittelgesetz.pdf.download.pdf/fedlex-data-admin-ch-eli-cc-2021-217-20210515-de-pdf-a-1.pdf)

### Inferences
- A QR page for a German prescription flower that shows only batch facts (origin, cultivator, harvest and packaging dates, COA values, irradiation status, recall status), reached by scanning a pack the patient already holds, sits on the information side of the MSD line. Adding effect descriptions, indications, strain "experience" language, rankings, ratings, discounts or links to prescribers moves it toward advertising. This is an inference from the cited case law, not a holding on QR pages; it needs German pharma-law counsel.
- The "pull" character (patient scans after dispensing) and third-party operation without a sales interest both help under the MSD test. A page operated or curated by the producer with selectable fields looks more like promotion.
- For KCanG clubs, anything beyond the statutory slip content shown to non-members risks the § 6 ban; a members-only view is safer.
- Swiss "neutral product information" language suggests the same factual-only design would fit the pilots and likely the CanPG.

### Gaps
- No decision found that addresses strain lists, price lists, pharmacy menus or QR/batch pages for cannabis flower specifically.
- The full BGH judgment text and press release No. 055/2026 were not opened; ECJ referral status is contradictory in the sources.
- Swiss advertising rules for medicinal cannabis (HMG/AWV) and the CanPG draft's advertising and labelling provisions were not retrieved.
- Definition of "Werbung" in § 2 KCanG not retrieved.

## 7. What patients/consumers want to know and whether transparency changes purchasing

### Takeaway
German patients today choose on price, THC percentage and peer reviews via comparison portals; average prescribed THC is above 24% and prices have fallen to about €3.77/g on the largest platform. The only quantitative evidence on transparency features is a Metrc-commissioned US survey (stated preference, non-probability sample): 88% say a state-issued QR would help distinguish regulated product, with a stated willingness to pay of $3.48 mean and $2 median.

### Cited Findings
- flowzz lists products with category, THC %, CBD %, price per gram and patient review counts (example: THC 29%, CBD 1%, from €3.99/g). — [flowzz product list](https://flowzz.com/product)
- Bloomwell data: average THC of prescribed flower stable above 24% (2025); flower above 25% THC "could soon make up half of all prescriptions"; around 86% say pharmacy cannabis is cheaper than or equal in price to illicit product (Bloomwell's own patients). — [Bloomwell Barometer (sleep disorders, April 2025)](https://25378200.fs1.hubspotusercontent-eu1.net/hubfs/25378200/Bloomwell%20Barometer%20-%20Schlafst%C3%B6rungen%20%5BDE%5D.pdf); [Bloomwell blog](https://bloomwell.de/blog/cannabis-barometer-patienten-warnen)
- "Transparency as Value" (Cannabis Public Policy Consulting, commissioned by Metrc, 2026): over 3,000 past-month consumers in 33 US jurisdictions; **88%** said the information would help distinguish regulated from unregulated products; 90% more likely to buy from licensed dispensaries and 89% more confident in the legal market with state-issued QR codes; among 1,886 valid answers, stated willingness to pay an extra **$3.48 mean, $2 median**. Authors caution it is a non-probability sample. — [report PDF](https://www.cannabispublicpolicyconsulting.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/TransparencyAsValue_8.18.pdf); [The Marijuana Herald](https://themarijuanaherald.com/2026/09/study-of-3000-cannabis-consumers-finds-90-more-likely-to-buy-from-licensed-dispensaries-89-more-confident-in-legal-market-with-state-issued-qr-codes/)
- New Mexico health department report on medical cannabis consumers: many participants wanted a QR code on packaging and said they would pay more for increased testing if the increase were reasonable (qualitative). — [NM Health report](https://www.nmhealth.org/publication/view/report/8779/)
- Analogy outside cannabis: a study of leafy greens found a median marginal willingness to pay of about $1.45 for blockchain-QR traceability access. — [PMC12507238](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12507238/)
- Swiss pilots: studies report that regulated supply promotes safer consumption and profile participants by motive (Geneva cluster study). — [International CBC](https://internationalcbc.com/swiss-study-regulating-cannabis-promotes-safer-consumption/); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/405038504_Who_Uses_Legal_Cannabis_and_Why_Cluster_Profiles_of_Participants_in_a_Swiss_Regulated_Sales_Pilot_Trial)

### Inferences
- The German market selects for high labelled THC at low price, which is exactly the incentive structure that produced potency inflation in the US. Verified potency would be the single most decision-relevant data point if patients trusted it more than the label.
- Evidence that transparency changes behaviour is stated-preference only, and the main study was paid for by the vendor of the feature. Treat willingness-to-pay figures as an upper bound.

### Gaps
- No German or Swiss survey found on what patients want to know (terpenes, irradiation, cultivator, harvest date). No data on German forum behaviour was retrieved.
- No independent, peer-reviewed study measuring actual purchases after QR/COA scans found; no scan-rate data.
- Swiss pilot participants' information needs and use of product information not retrieved.

## 8. Who would pay; regulators mandating consumer-facing traceability

### Takeaway
The only observed payment models are (a) licensees paying a regulator-mandated per-unit fee ($0.10 per code in New York) and (b) operators paying SaaS fees for track-and-trace where the QR page is a module (Cannavigia). No evidence was found of consumers, pharmacies, clubs or insurers paying for provenance data in Europe.

### Inferences
- The likeliest payers in Germany are **importers/wholesalers and their Qualified Persons**, who now need defensible evidence of where and under what standard flower was grown and processed; the consumer page is a by-product of data they must assemble anyway.
- Price pressure (€3.77/g retail) leaves little margin for a per-unit fee unless mandated or unless verified product commands a premium. At 300 t a year in 5–10 g packs (about 30–60 million units, my arithmetic, not sourced), a New York-style fee of about €0.10 would be a €3–6 m a year pool for all of Germany.
- Regulators are a channel more than a payer: the realistic route to scale is being named in, or compatible with, a mandated system (Swiss CanPG, a KCanG § 21(4) ordinance, Dutch post-experiment law).
- No sign that statutory health insurers care: most German volume is self-pay.

### Gaps
- No evidence on willingness to pay by cultivators, importers, pharmacies or clubs in Europe; no Cannavigia pricing retrieved.
- No evidence found that any European regulator plans to mandate consumer-facing traceability; the CanPG draft's track-and-trace and labelling articles were not retrieved and should be read directly.
- Insurer interest: no sources found.
