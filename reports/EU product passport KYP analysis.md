# EU product passports check format, not truth

The EU Digital Product Passport (DPP) is real infrastructure with almost no obligations attached yet, and it does not cover cannabis. The central registry went live on 20 July 2026 and eight harmonised standards exist, but as of the latest sources (October 2026) no product group is under a passport duty through the Ecodesign regulation (ESPR). The first hard date is the battery passport on 18 February 2027, and ESPR passports for ordinary goods arrive from mid-2028 at the earliest. Medicines, food and living plants are excluded from the ESPR by law, so medical cannabis and CBD food will never receive an EU passport duty, and adult-use flower is on no plan. The system also checks that passport data is present and well-formed, not that it is true. That leaves independent verification of content as the open commercial space, which is where "Know Your Product" (KYP) would sit. The evidence supports the founder's thesis on the supply side: regulated goods already generate verified batch data that never travels downstream, and European cannabis has documented integrity problems upstream of the pharmacy. It undermines the thesis on the demand side: measured consumer scan rates are 0.1% to about 1%, every verification layer that became standard did so through a regulator or a dominant buyer, and "KYC without the pain" misreads why KYC exists at all, which is legal compulsion. The defensible position is a neutral attestation layer that importers, pharmacies and regulators rely on and consumers can inspect, built on DPP-compatible identifiers, not a consumer app or a new trust mark. One caveat applies to everything below: the researchers could run web searches but could almost never open primary pages, so most findings rest on search summaries of law-firm notes, trade press and vendor blogs.

## How far this evidence can be trusted

All six research streams worked from search-result extracts on 13 November 2026. EUR-Lex, Commission pages, the standards bodies, court texts and vendor sites (including cannavigia.com) could not be opened. Three consequences follow. Article-level statements about the ESPR were written from the adopted text as the researchers knew it and were not re-read; paragraph numbers need checking before external use. The 2026 developments (registry rules, standards publication, slipping timelines) come largely from vendor trackers that sometimes contradict each other. And several of the most-quoted consumer figures were commissioned by an interested party, which the text flags each time.

Four specific points were unresolved at the cut-off. Nobody could confirm whether the iron and steel delegated act was adopted between 9 October and 13 November 2026. Sources disagree on whether the access-rights standard EN 18239 is published. No source could confirm that the EU registry accepts real production registrations. And trademark registers were not queried for "Know Your Product" or "KYP". Where this report draws its own conclusions from sourced facts, it says so.

## The passport system is built, but the obligations are years out

The ESPR, Regulation (EU) 2024/1781, entered into force on 18 July 2024 as a framework law. It defines the architecture and leaves every product duty to later delegated acts ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1781)). The architecture has six parts: a data carrier on the product, a persistent unique identifier, passport data held by the company or its service provider, a mandatory back-up copy at an independent "DPP service provider", a central EU registry of identifiers, and an automated customs check against that registry. Each delegated act then fixes the data fields, the granularity (model, batch or item), who may see which data, and how long the passport must stay available. The regulation demands open standards and portability "without vendor lock-in", which means the hosting layer is designed to be a commodity.

The registry is the one piece in production. Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1778 was adopted on 16 July 2026 and entered into force on 6 August; the Commission launched the registry with a test environment on 20 July ([Commission](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/news/digital-product-passport-registry-now-live-2026-07-20_en), [fn.legal](https://fn.legal/en/implementing-regulation-on-the-product-passport-register/)). It stores identifiers and pointers, not passport content ([GS1 UK](https://www.gs1uk.org/insights/news/EU-Digital-Product-Passport-Registry-goes-live)). CMS stresses that the launch "is not, by itself, a new registration obligation" ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/int/legal-updates/key-development-in-the-eu-digital-product-passport-launch-of-the-dpp-registry)).

The obligations lag well behind. A tracker dated 9 October 2026 reports that **no ESPR delegated act with passport requirements had been published for any product group** ([dppatlas](https://dppatlas.eu/insights/dpp-update-october-2026), secondary only). Iron and steel, the first act in the 2025–2030 working plan, went through consultation from 20 May to 12 August 2026 and is indicated for Q4 2026. Duties apply at least 18 months after an act enters into force ([One Click LCA](https://oneclicklca.com/en-gb/resources/articles/first-espr-working-plan)), so mid-2028 is the earliest realistic ESPR passport duty. The sector laws that reuse the same infrastructure are moving faster than the ESPR itself.

Three points in this table matter for KYP. First, the vendor line that "the DPP arrives in 2026/2027" is wrong for everything except batteries. Second, the simplification omnibus packages have not cut the DPP; the Commission treats it as the replacement for other reporting duties, so the political risk is delay, not repeal ([passportcraft](https://passportcraft.com/insights/eu-omnibus-dpp-obligations-unchanged), secondary only). Third, the act that would define and possibly certify DPP service providers is the most important pending decision for any new entrant, and its certification model is unpublished. Industry body Orgalim argued for self-assessment as the rule ([Orgalim](https://orgalim.eu/wp-content/uploads/Orgalim_Consultation-on-DPP-service-providers.pdf)). Until it is adopted, any vendor claiming to be a "certified DPP service provider" is ahead of the law.

Enforcement is national. ESPR Article 74 sets no fine amounts and tells Member States to set penalties; the "5% of turnover" figures on vendor sites are not in the regulation ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1781)). The real lever is customs: release for free circulation will depend on a valid registration identifier. No enforcement has happened anywhere yet. Outside the EU, Switzerland has no DPP statute and follows through its exporters and GS1 Switzerland ([Helbling](https://helbling.ch/en/insights/how-companies-can-prepare-for-the-digital-product-passport-dpp-now)), and the UK opened a call for evidence on a "digital product record" on 27 July 2026 ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/digital-product-records-uk-call-for-evidence-and-eu-dpp-update)). A Swiss company therefore faces no domestic passport mandate.

## The standards settle identifiers and signatures and leave trust open

The technical design is a federated pointer system. A code on the product resolves to the company's passport; the company or its provider serves public data openly and restricted data after authentication; an independent provider mirrors the data; the EU registry holds the identifier and the back-up pointer; and an EU web portal acts as resolver of last resort ([smartlinks on CIRPASS-2](https://www.smartlinks.app/resources/cirpass-reference-architecture), secondary). The CIRPASS-2 reference architecture published in June 2026 is explicitly exploratory, not binding ([CIRPASS-2](https://cirpass2.eu/project-results/)).

Eight standards from CEN/CENELEC carry the detail. Six were published on 27 May 2026 covering identifiers (EN 18219), data carriers (EN 18220), exchange protocols (EN 18216), storage and persistence (EN 18221), APIs (EN 18222) and interoperability (EN 18223) ([CDX](https://public.cdxsystem.com/en/web/cdx/w/first-european-standards-for-the-digital-product-passport-published)). Secondary sources report EN 18246 (data authentication and integrity) and EN 18239 (access rights) as published on 16 September 2026 ([DPPify](https://www.dppify.com/blog/dpp-standards-en-18239-18246-published)); one research stream could not confirm EN 18239. No clause of any standard was read.

For a builder, four design facts follow from what the sources report. **Identifiers are plural.** EN 18219 does not mandate GS1. It admits several schemes rooted in ISO/IEC 15459, with GS1 Digital Link as the de facto retail choice ([Regen Studio](https://www.regenstudio.world/blog/dpp-system-standards/), secondary). **The baseline carrier is a plain QR code.** EN 18220 requires at least one free, smartphone-readable carrier, which is trivially copyable ([idpp.net](https://idpp.net/en/guides/dpp-standards-guide), secondary). The standards address data authenticity, not physical authenticity.

**Blockchain is not required anywhere.** The EU Blockchain Observatory itself advises against NFT-based passports because public ledgers conflict with business confidentiality ([EUBOF](https://blockchain-observatory.ec.europa.eu/document/download/b6e3c85c-43c1-405b-aba8-e49a71249ef7_en?filename=EUBOF_DPP_report.pdf)). **Event data has a mature vocabulary.** GS1 EPCIS 2.0 is standardised as ISO/IEC 19987:2024 and records what happened to an object, where and when ([GS1](https://www.gs1.org/epcis)). No DPP standard makes it mandatory, but it is the natural bridge from a seed-to-sale system to passport content.

Verification is where the stack stops. EN 18246 accepts several signature mechanisms, including W3C Verifiable Credentials and eIDAS attestations ([Trustrace](https://trustrace.com/knowledge-hub/the-final-two-european-dpp-standards-are-here-what-it-means-for-fashion-and-apparel-brands), secondary). A signature proves who issued a statement and that it was not altered. It does not prove the statement is true, and the registry's automated checks cover structure and completeness only. Euroconsumers put the criticism plainly in June 2026: the rules "establish a formal validation regime" that is "insufficient to underpin a credible data quality system" ([Euroconsumers](https://www.euroconsumers.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Euroconsumers-Feedback-on-Commission-Draft-Implementing-Regulation-on-the-Digital-Product-Passport-Registry.pdf)).

The UN Transparency Protocol (UNTP) offers the closest blueprint for "KYC for products". Its chain runs from the manufacturer's passport, to a conformity credential issued by an accredited assessor, to an identity anchor issued by a business register ([UNTP](https://spec-untp-fbb45f.opensource.unicc.org/docs/0.7.0/design-patterns/TrustGraphs/)). Two caveats apply. UNTP was at version 0.7 with 1.0 delayed, and no 1.0 release was found. And its own working group notes that proof of an assessor's accreditation "today is typically a PDF or web link" ([UNTP Conformity Group](https://untp.unece.org/docs/governance/meetings/conformityGroup/)). The link between a signing key and an accredited lab's scope is the least digitised step everywhere. Catena-X, the only data space in real production, likewise lists data validation and verification as unfinished work ([Catena-X](https://catena-x.net/use-case-cluster/digital-product-passport/)). No source showed an accredited lab issuing certificates of analysis as verifiable credentials at scale.

Two further technical findings constrain a consumer-facing product. Anti-cloning needs a second factor beyond the QR code, and neither option is absolute: cryptographic NFC chips prove the chip is genuine, not the contents, and printed copy-detection patterns have been cloned by machine-learning attacks under some conditions ([arXiv 2110.02176](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02176)). And confidentiality is handled by access tiers, not cryptography; the practical way to "prove the claim and hide the supplier" is attestation by a trusted third party who sees the confidential evidence. That last point is the researchers' inference, though it matches UNTP's design.

## Cannabis and most regulated consumables sit outside the passport

ESPR Article 1(2) excludes food, feed, human and veterinary medicinal products, living plants, and plant products relating to reproduction ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1781), [GS1](https://support.gs1.org/support/solutions/articles/43000758757-which-products-are-out-of-scope-for-the-dpp-initiative-)). Applied to cannabis, the result is clear for the regulated core and untested at the edge. **Medical cannabis** is a medicinal product and is excluded. **CBD oils, edibles and hemp food** are food and are excluded, as are plants, clones and seeds. **Adult-use flower** is not obviously excluded, but it has no harmonised EU market and appears in no working plan; no commentary on its classification was found, so this is the researchers' own legal reasoning. Hemp textiles and construction materials fall under the textile and construction passport rules.

A targeted search found no source discussing cannabis, hemp or CBD in relation to the DPP. For KYP's first use case the DPP is therefore a technical template and a source of credibility, not a legal driver.

The same holds for most highly regulated goods. A comparison of the mandatory regimes shows that every one stops at an authority or the last licensed professional, and none gives the consumer a check on the unit in their hand.

| Regime | Granularity | Who pays and runs it | What the consumer can check |
|---|---|---|---|

| Pharmacy-compounded medicines (incl. German cannabis flower) | None mandated | n/a | Nothing |
| EU tobacco track-and-trace | Unit pack | State-appointed code issuers; industry-contracted repositories | Visible security feature only |
| Vapes, nicotine pouches | None at EU level | n/a | Nothing |
| Medical devices (EUDAMED) | Model in database | Commission | Model and certificate, not the unit |

Sources for the table: [Delegated Regulation 2016/161](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_del/2016/161/oj), [Implementing Regulation 2018/574](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2018/574/oj), [EUDAMED](https://ec.europa.eu/tools/eudamed), [Regulation 178/2002](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2002/178/oj), [Aura](https://auraconsortium.com/?p=2348). Several rows rest on the legal texts as recalled, not re-read.

These regimes carry four lessons that bear directly on KYP.

**Governance decides credibility.** The tobacco system is paid for and partly selected by industry, and its independence has been attacked by academics and, in September 2026, by the European Court of Auditors ([Tobacco Control](https://tobaccocontrol.bmj.com/content/29/e1/e56), [Health Policy Watch](https://healthpolicy-watch.news/europe-failing-illegal-tobacco/)). A repository paid for by the brands it verifies will meet the same objection.

**Data errors look like fraud.** Early in the medicines system about 3% of scans raised false alerts, mostly from upload and scanner errors, and pharmacies carried the burden ([GMP-compliance.org](https://www.gmp-compliance.org/gmp-news/track-trace-emvo-stakeholders-call-for-inspections)). A consumer-facing "not verified" caused by a supplier's typo is a reputational event.

**Mandates slip and contracts bite.** EUDAMED, the deforestation regulation, the US food traceability rule and the G7 diamond scheme were all delayed in 2025–2026, while large retailers wrote the US food rule into supplier contracts ahead of the legal date ([The Packer](https://www.thepacker.com/news/food-safety/what-you-need-know-about-fsma-204-delay)).

**A mandated QR page is not a marketing channel.** The wine e-label page may carry no advertising and may not track users ([wein.plus](https://magazine.wein.plus/this-is-how-nutritional-value-and-ingredient-information-must-look-on-the-label-all-details-of-the-eu-regulation-from-december)).

The licensed-medicine, tobacco, explosives and firearms lanes are closed: data access is fixed by law and a third party cannot re-expose it. The open lanes are the goods that fall between regimes. These are compounded and unlicensed medicines, nicotine pouches and vapes until the tobacco directive revision takes effect (a vendor source suggests 2028), cosmetics and supplements, origin-premium foods and alcohol, and unit-level authentication on top of model-level registries. German cannabis flower is the largest example of the first lane. It is dispensed as a pharmacy preparation, and compounded preparations fall outside the medicines directive and therefore outside serialisation ([CMS](https://cms.law/de/deu/legal-updates/cannabisblueten-ausgangsstoff-oder-bereits-arzneimittel)). No source states the securPharm exemption explicitly; it follows from the directive's scope.

One legal change narrows what KYP may claim. Since 27 September 2026 the Empowering Consumers Directive bans sustainability labels that lack a certification scheme, while the Green Claims Directive, which would have created a statutory claim-verifier role, is listed for withdrawal ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/11/07/the-green-claims-directive-is-dead-but-what-will-be-the-net-effect-of-zero-toler-102o8n1)). A badge limited to authenticity and provenance facts is less exposed than one implying quality or sustainability.

## Cannabis has a real integrity gap and a narrow legal corridor

### The market is German medical flower; Switzerland is the home base

Germany dominates by volume. The regulator's corrected figure for Q1 2026 is **67.6 tonnes** of imported medical flower, the highest quarter on record ([krautinvest](https://krautinvest.de/cannabis-importe-in-q1-2026-die-gruende-fuer-die-33-prozent-korrektur/)), and the health ministry forecast 300 tonnes for 2026 ([hanf-magazin](https://www.hanf-magazin.com/recht/rechtslage-deutschland/300-tonnen-importe-linnemann-draengt-auf-schaerfere-medizinalcannabis-regeln/)). Prohibition Partners puts the German medical market at about USD 997 million in 2025 ([Prohibition Partners](https://prohibitionpartners.com/2026/03/26/five-takeaways-from-the-global-medical-cannabis-market-review-2026/)). This is a prescription medicine dispensed by pharmacies. Cultivation clubs supply about 3.5% of the market, and commercial adult-use retail is effectively dead: the federal agency rejected 29 research projects and approved 4 ([Bundestag Drs. 21/8143](https://dserver.bundestag.de/btd/21/081/2108143.pdf)).

That volume is politically exposed. A pending amendment would require in-person prescription and ban pharmacy mail-order. It had not been adopted as of August–October 2026 ([jiroo](https://jiroo.de/medcang-radar/)), and Prohibition Partners warns of "a significant decline" if it passes ([Business of Cannabis](https://businessofcannabis.com/is-the-german-cannabis-boom-slowing-down-or-simply-normalising/)).

Switzerland is the only European market where track-and-trace of adult-use product is mandatory, and Cannavigia describes itself as the exclusive software provider for all seven pilot trials ([Cannavigia](https://www.cannavigia.com/pilot-projects)). The base is small, at about 10,400 participants ([IDPC](https://idpc.net/publications/2026/02/analysis-of-results-from-cannabis-pilot-trials-in-swiss-cities)). Full legalisation was sent back to a sub-commission in May 2026 with no confirmed timetable ([BAG](https://www.bag.admin.ch/de/neues-cannabisproduktegesetz)).

No European regime requires a consumer-facing digital record. German clubs must hand over a paper slip with six data points, including harvest date and average THC and CBD content ([§ 21 KCanG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/kcang/__21.html)). Swiss pilots require neutral product information on a sealed pack, with producers determining THC content under self-control ([BAG](https://www.bag.admin.ch/de/ausnahmebewilligungen-im-zusammenhang-mit-pilotversuchen-nach-artikel-8a-betmg)).

### The documented problems are upstream provenance in Europe and potency in America

The strongest quantified failure is North American. In a 2025 University of Colorado study of 277 products, only **56.7% of flower** tested within 15% of the labelled THC ([CU Boulder](https://www.colorado.edu/today/2025/07/03/can-weed-labels-be-trusted-study-shows-it-depends-what-youre-buying)). Identical samples sent to different Colorado labs varied by up to 38% ([MJBizDaily](https://mjbizdaily.com/?p=407790)), and several California and Nevada labs lost licences for inflating potency ([MJBizDaily](https://mjbizdaily.com/california-regulators-revoke-marijuana-testing-lab-license-for-faking-results/)).

No comparable German study exists. The German evidence is batch recalls and doubts about certification. Trade sources allege "GMP washing", where flower grown to basic agricultural standards passes through an EU-GMP facility to gain a certificate ([MFLRC](https://mflrc.com/article/gmp-washing-germany-cannabis-drying-rule)); this is an allegation, not an enforcement finding. In one documented case, inspectors found a Canadian producer non-compliant after several of its batches had already been imported, and German authorities are now pushing GMP responsibility back to harvest ([StratCann](https://stratcann.com/news/germanys-bfarm-clarifies-cultivar-approval-as-eu-gmp-scrutiny-increases/)).

This supports the founder's core design choice. The European integrity gap sits upstream of the pharmacy: where flower was grown and dried, and whether the certificate covers those steps. A track-and-trace vendor holds that data; a certificate of analysis does not. It also sets a limit. A layer that only republishes the producer's own lab certificate inherits the lab-shopping problem. The founder should not assume German patients face American-scale mislabelling without data.

### Advertising law permits facts and little else

German law bans advertising of prescription medicines to the public. On 26 March 2026 the Federal Court of Justice applied the ban to a cannabis telemedicine platform even though no brand was named ([Ärzte Zeitung](https://www.aerztezeitung.de/Wirtschaft/BGH-untersagt-Werbung-fuer-aerztliche-Behandlungen-mit-medizinischem-Cannabis-462491.html)). The EU court's MSD ruling keeps purely informational content lawful, judged by overall presentation ([it-recht-kanzlei](https://www.it-recht-kanzlei.de/infos-verschreibungspflchtige-medikamente-werbung-internet.html)). The club law bans all advertising for cannabis.

The researchers infer that a page showing only batch facts, reached by scanning a pack the patient already holds, sits on the information side of that line. Effect descriptions, rankings, ratings or links to prescribers move it toward advertising. Third-party operation without a sales interest helps; a producer-curated page with selectable fields looks like promotion. No decision addresses QR or batch pages for cannabis, so this needs German pharmaceutical-law counsel.

### Who pays is unproven

The one hard price point for mandated consumer traceability is New York. From 1 March 2026 every unit must carry a state-system QR code, and licensees pay **USD 0.10 per code** ([NY OCM](https://cannabis.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2026/02/sts-bulletin-14.pdf), [Distru](https://www.distru.com/metrc-bulletins/ny-metrc-retail-item-id-deadlines-for-2026)). In California the same scheme is voluntary and covers about 20% of weekly volume ([Cannabis Equipment News](https://www.cannabisequipmentnews.com/processing/news/22943414/metrc-retail-id-achieves-1-million-weekly-run-rate-of-qr-codes)). Applied to Germany's forecast 300 tonnes in 5–10 g packs, a fee of about EUR 0.10 yields roughly EUR 3–6 million a year for the whole country. That is the researchers' arithmetic, not a sourced estimate, and it is a small pool.

No evidence was found of consumers, pharmacies, clubs or insurers paying for provenance data in Europe. The likeliest payers are importers, wholesalers and their Qualified Persons, who now need defensible evidence of a batch's history. That too is an inference. Germany has no central cannabis track-and-trace, though the Bundestag lobby register shows one being proposed ([Lobbyregister](https://www.lobbyregister.bundestag.de/media/15/b3/843074/Lobbyregister-Regelungsvorhaben-Detailansicht-RV0021205-2026-09-17_16-41-46.pdf)).

## Publishing passports is crowded and cheap; verifying them is thin

More than 50 vendors publish and host passports. Gartner's first DPP quadrant (6 July 2026) names TrusTrace, Circulor, Kezzler, Narravero, Siemens and Tilkal as "market shapers", though this is known only through vendor press releases because the report is paywalled ([TrusTrace](https://trustrace.com/newsroom/trustrace-named-a-market-shaper-in-gartner-emerging-market-quadrant-for-digital-product-passport)). The market sorts into eight archetypes.

| Archetype | Examples | What they sell |
|---|---|---|
| Traceability platforms with DPP added | TrusTrace, Retraced, Circulor, Circularise | Supplier-portal data collection, mostly fashion and batteries |
| Product identity and consumer engagement | EON, Arianee, Avery Dennison, Narravero | Item-level IDs and branded scan experiences |
| Serialisation and authentication | Kezzler, Scantrust, Antares Vision, SICPA, Inexto | Line-level codes and event data for pharma, tobacco, food |
| Verifiable-credential infrastructure | Spherity, Veribl | Signing and verification of passport data |
| Compliance and life-cycle suites | Assent (acquired iPoint, July 2026), Siemens | Product-compliance data foundations |
| Product-information add-ons | AtroPIM, Productsup with Protokol | Passports generated from catalogue data |
| Self-service SME tools | DPP-Tool, Buyerdock, Arkive | Passport publishing from EUR 9 a month |
| Testing and certification groups | DNV, SGS, Intertek | Mostly advisory; DNV has a verified-claims product |

Three patterns stand out. First, data entry is overwhelmingly self-declared, and "verified" in vendor copy usually means a document upload or a signature on the declarant's own data. This is the researchers' reading across vendors, not a measured finding. Second, capital is modest: the largest verifiable rounds are USD 16–24 million (TrusTrace's USD 24 million in January 2024 is the biggest confirmed) ([FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.uk/news/business/trustrace-secures-24-million-us-dollar-growth-investment/2024011873651)). Third, publishing is already a commodity at the low end, with per-SKU activation at EUR 0.25–1.00 ([GetApp](https://www.getapp.ca/software/2103779/dpp-cloud)). No public price exists for verification as a separate line item. Market-size forecasts for 2030 range from about USD 0.7 billion ([GII Korea](https://m.giikorea.co.kr/report/go1739103-digital-product-passport-software.html)) to USD 1.8 billion ([MarketsandMarkets](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/01/16/3220379/0/en/Digital-Product-Passport-DPP-Market-Surges-to-1-780-5-million-by-2030-CAGR-45-7.html)), with outliers near USD 10 billion by 2035. They come from commercial report vendors with differing scopes and are not usable for planning.

Verification, where it exists, comes in three kinds: signature integrity (Spherity, Veribl), accredited attestation (DNV, labs) and physical forensics (Oritain). No vendor found combines them for one regulated category, and no search result showed any firm positioning as "KYC for products". That is a negative finding from limited searches. The testing groups hold the accreditation that gives an attestation legal weight but have not productised per-batch machine-verifiable credentials; the credential vendors have the technology but no accreditation. Serialisation incumbents are buying in credential capability, as Antares Vision did by reselling Spherity ([WhatTheyThink](https://whattheythink.com/news/128567-antares-vision-group-spherity-expand-partnership-reseller-agreement-deliver-dscsa-atp-credentials/)).

In cannabis, the field is small. Cannavigia's own Product Pass is already a batch-level QR page fed from the seed-to-sale platform, sold on the business problem of repeated certificate requests, with the operator choosing which fields are visible and a consumer tier listed as "tbd" ([Cannavigia](https://www.cannavigia.com/productpass-module)). An operator-controlled page where the operator chooses what to show is a marketing page, not a verification. Other European offers are a patient-entitlement credential (Ubirch with Cansativa) and a private seal (CannaTrust, current status unverified). In Germany the de facto consumer information layer is comparison portals such as flowzz, which show price, THC percentage and peer reviews and hold no verified batch data ([flowzz](https://flowzz.com/product)). In the US, the state-backed Metrc code is displacing private item-level schemes, and older private players show no recent activity.

The previous generation of provenance ventures failed commercially, not technically. TradeLens closed in 2022 because competitors would not join a platform controlled by Maersk ([IMD](https://www.imd.org/ibyimd/supply-chain/where-does-blockchain-go-from-here/)). Everledger went into administration in 2023 ([Ledger Insights](https://www.ledgerinsights.com/everledger-bankruptcy-esg-blockchain-traceability/)). IBM withdrew the suite containing Food Trust from marketing in May 2025 ([IBM](https://www.ibm.com/support/pages/node/7180915)). The survivor, Provenance, moved from blockchain tracing to verified claims embedded at retailers' point of sale.

## Consumer evidence is the weakest link in the thesis

### People say they scan and mostly do not

Measured scan rates for information QR codes are tiny. A pilot cited by WHO/Europe found **0.26% of shoppers** scanned alcohol-label codes for health information ([WHO/Europe](https://www.who.int/europe/news-room/14-02-2025-alcohol-labels-should-warn-of-cancer-risk--says-new-who-europe-report)); industry disputed its validity, and its method was not retrieved. A US field experiment on oysters found 1.2% ([AgEcon Search](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/287977)). Surveys tell the opposite story: 75% of consumers in five EU countries say they have scanned a food or drink QR code, in a survey commissioned by the spirits industry ([spiritsEUROPE](https://spirits.eu/media/press-releases/digital-labels-new-survey-shows-a-majority-of-european-consumers-regularly-use-e-labels-to-access-product-information)). No fashion DPP pilot, GS1 retail pilot or cannabis programme has published scan rates.

### Stated willingness to pay shrinks in real stores

Choice experiments reliably find a premium for traceability, but it is among the lowest-valued credence attributes. A French trial across 60 supermarkets found label effects about 17 times smaller than in comparable lab studies ([Dubois et al.](https://www.tse-fr.eu/node/120536)). Reading more traceability detail behind a QR code did not raise willingness to pay ([British Food Journal](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/BFJ-09-2023-0812/full/html)). Independent verification does not consistently beat a brand's own claim in experiments, and 15% of US supplement users said they look for a seal that does not exist ([Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/health/supplements/what-to-know-before-you-take-that-supplement-a8255329318)).

The one cannabis-specific figure is a US survey commissioned by Metrc, the vendor of the feature. In it, 88% said a state-issued QR would help distinguish regulated product, and stated willingness to pay was USD 2 at the median ([CPPC report](https://www.cannabispublicpolicyconsulting.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/TransparencyAsValue_8.18.pdf)). It is a non-probability sample and stated preference; treat it as an upper bound. No German or Swiss survey on what patients want to know was found.

### What works is a simple signal, and it works mainly on producers

Information changes behaviour when it is compressed into one comparable signal at the point of choice and when it can be negative. Nutri-Score was the best performer in the French trial yet improved basket quality by only 2.5%. The scoring app Yuka led Intermarché to reformulate 900 products ([Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2019/09/20/french-supermarket-chain-to-remove-additives-using-phone-app-that-rates-products)). The larger documented effect of public scores is on suppliers, not shoppers.

There is one genuine support for the thesis here. Verification is worth most where the attribute is safety- or value-critical, fraud is known, and the buyer cannot check. Cannabis potency and contaminants fit that description. Distrust of unverified claims is also rising: 62% of consumers felt organisations were greenwashing in 2025 ([Capgemini](https://capgemini.turtl.co/story/insights-research-library-sustainability-trends-2025/page/3)). Cannabis is also bought at a counter with staff mediation, so scanning behaviour could differ from supermarkets. That is a hypothesis; no measured data exists.

### "KYC without the pain" misreads KYC

KYC exists because the law puts a non-delegable duty and liability on a defined gatekeeper. Nobody buys it voluntarily. It costs financial institutions in EMEA about USD 85 billion a year, per a LexisNexis-commissioned study ([LexisNexis](https://risk.lexisnexis.com/global/de/about-us/press-room/press-release/20240306-true-cost-of-compliance)), and a corporate review averages USD 2,598 ([A-Team Insight](https://a-teaminsight.com/blog/fenergo-study-reveals-true-cost-of-kyc)). Attempts to share the work mostly failed. Singapore's regulator shelved its KYC utility because "the economics did not work out" ([Asian Private Banker](https://asianprivatebanker.com/private-wealth/regulations/mas-not-succeeded-building-kyc-utility-ravi-menon/)), and McKinsey attributes such failures to misaligned incentives and the relying party remaining liable ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/financial-services/our-insights/banking-matters/a-kyc-aml-utility-driving-scale-efficiency-and-effectiveness)).

What did scale is narrower: standard identifiers under neutral governance and reusable credentials. The Legal Entity Identifier passed 3.35 million records, though only 61.9% were in good standing in Q1 2026 ([GLEIF](https://www.gleif.org/lei-data/global-lei-index/download-global-lei-system-business-reports/download-global-lei-system-business-report-q1-2026/q1-2026-quarterly_business_report.pdf)). The usable analogy for KYP is therefore "issuer-signed, reusable batch credentials", not a shared database of verdicts.

The pattern across precedents is consistent. Verification became standard when a law or a gatekeeping buyer required it: Amazon made third-party testing a listing condition for supplement categories in 2024 ([Nutraceuticals World](https://www.nutraceuticalsworld.com/exclusives/amazon-supplement-policy-update-third-party-verification-required-from-nsf-ul-or-eurofins/)), CVS has required it since 2019, and EcoVadis earns 52% of revenue from suppliers whose customers demand a rating ([EcoVadis](https://ecovadis.com/trust-center/esma/)). The research found no case where it became standard through consumer demand alone. EU law is already borrowing KYC logic for commerce, since the Digital Services Act obliges marketplaces to "know your business customer" ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/compliance/1367256/online-marketplaces-and-the-digital-services-act-understanding-trader-traceability)), but the compulsion sits on the marketplace.

The producer-pays model also has a poor integrity record. A Pakistani factory burned down three weeks after receiving a social-audit certificate, killing at least 250 workers ([Clean Clothes Campaign](https://cleanclothes.org/news/2020/faulty-pakistan-factory-audit-italian-social-auditor-rina-yet-again-disregards-families-harmed-by-textile-factory-fire-)). The trusted models separate the verifier from the paying producer: Stiftung Warentest licenses its logo at arm's length through a separate non-profit and re-tests about 10% of products that advertise with it ([test.de](https://www.test.de/unternehmen/werbung-5016972-0/)).

## What this implies for KYP's positioning

**Position as the verification layer the DPP lacks, for goods the DPP does not cover.** The EU has built identifiers, carriers, registries and signature formats and has left content truth to the market. Most highly regulated consumables are excluded from, or unscheduled in, the passport programme for this decade. KYP should adopt the DPP stack for compatibility (GS1 Digital Link or other ISO-rooted identifiers, EPCIS events, EN 18246-style signed data) and should not present itself as a DPP compliance product, because for cannabis no such duty exists.

**Sell to the party that carries liability; show the consumer the result.** The evidence points to importers, wholesalers, Qualified Persons, pharmacies and regulators as the users with a problem and a budget. The consumer view is what distinguishes KYP from supplier-compliance software and what a regulator can point to. It should be budgeted as a cost of legitimacy until measured usage says otherwise.

**Separate KYP's governance from Cannavigia and from the paying producer.** This is the hardest implication. Cannavigia's data is KYP's main asset, because it is created under legal duty and inspection and so partly answers the "garbage in" problem. But a verifier owned by the track-and-trace vendor and paid by the verified producers repeats the TradeLens and tobacco objections. Who chooses what is tested, who holds the evidence, and whether failures are published must sit outside the producer's control. KYP also needs to decide whether it is a data intermediary that aggregates accredited parties' attestations, or an accredited certifier itself with the cost and conflict rules that brings.

**Attest facts, not quality.** Provenance, custody, test results with named accredited issuers, recall status and expiry are defensible under German advertising law and the new unfair-practices rules. Scores implying quality, effect or sustainability are not, without a certification scheme behind them. This sits in tension with the consumer evidence, which favours one graded signal over raw data. A pass/fail "verified against stated facts" flag with negatives shown as prominently as positives is the narrow overlap.

**Expect adverse selection under voluntary opt-in.** The German market selects for high labelled THC at low price. Honest verified potency can look worse than a competitor's inflated label, so brand-by-brand adoption punishes the honest. Adoption at the level of a regulator, pilot operator or pharmacy chain avoids this.

**Treat the brand name as an analogy, not a category.** "Know Your Product" is a defined obligation in Canadian securities regulation ([CIRO](https://www.ciro.ca/news-room/publications/product-due-diligence-and-know-your-product)). Among identity vendors "KYP" means "Know Your Patient", including for online pharmacies ([Persona](https://withpersona.com/blog/know-your-patient-kyp-how-to-mitigate-healthcare-fraud)), which is awkward in cannabis specifically. A UK risk firm trades as KYP ("Know Your Partner"). The phrase is legible to investors and regulators and no provenance player owns it, but it is descriptive, and no trademark clearance has been done.

## Open questions to resolve before building

| Question | Why it decides the design | How to resolve |
|---|---|---|
| Who is compelled, and by whom? | Without a mandate or a contractual requirement there is no KYC-like pull | Interview German importers, Qualified Persons and pharmacy groups; read the Swiss draft law's tracking and labelling articles directly |
| Who carries liability when a "verified" batch is wrong? | If relying parties stay fully liable they re-verify and the saving disappears | Legal opinion covering the Product Liability Directive (transposition due 9 December 2026) and unfair-practices law |
| Who attests, and are they accredited? | Republishing producer certificates inherits lab-shopping | Establish whether labs serving European cannabis will issue signed per-batch results; decide intermediary versus certifier role |
| Can KYP be credibly neutral while linked to Cannavigia? | Competitors' producers and other track-and-trace vendors must be able to join | Design ownership, data custody and audit rights before product; test with a non-Cannavigia operator |
| What does Cannavigia's data actually prove? | The thesis rests on upstream provenance the certificate lacks | Internal audit of Product Pass fields, coverage of imported flower, and which entries are operator-declared versus system-verified |
| Is the batch page lawful in Germany? | The advertising ban reaches platforms; no ruling covers batch pages | German pharmaceutical-law counsel on a concrete mock-up |
| Do patients use it? | No measured cannabis scan data exists anywhere | A pilot with one pharmacy or Swiss trial that measures real scans, not survey intent |
| Is there a German label-accuracy problem? | The potency evidence is American | Commission an independent test of labelled versus measured values on German pharmacy flower |
| What will the service-provider act and UNTP 1.0 require? | They set the entry bar and the credential format | Track adoption; obtain and read EN 18246 and EN 18239 |
| Is the name clear to use? | Descriptive phrase, existing "KYP" users in adjacent classes | Trademark clearance at EUIPO, Swissreg, UKIPO and USPTO |
| Which second category? | The open lanes differ in payer and timing | Compare supplements, cosmetics and nicotine pouches on who can impose a requirement by contract |

## Conclusion

The research inverts the founder's framing in one respect and confirms it in another. The pain of KYC is the price of its compulsion, and removing the pain removes the pull; KYP needs its own source of compulsion and should design for the gatekeeper first. But the founder's instinct to build only on real track-and-trace and compliance data is the right answer to the problem that sank the last generation of provenance ventures and that the EU's own passport system has declined to solve. The standards now make issuer-signed, portable batch attestations technically ordinary. What remains scarce is an accountable party whose attestations others accept.

Timing favours a deliberate start. The service-provider rules, UNTP 1.0, the German medical cannabis amendment and the Swiss law are all unresolved, and the battery passport in February 2027 will be the first real test of how verification is handled in practice. The cheapest useful step is not a platform. It is a single measured pilot with one relying party, one accredited lab and Cannavigia's batch data, set up to answer the questions above with observed behaviour instead of stated intent.
